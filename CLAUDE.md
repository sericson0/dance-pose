# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

TAngle (*Tango + Angle*) — an interactive 3D tool for teaching Argentine tango: pose two dancers (leader/follower) with anatomically constrained joints, view skeleton/body/muscle layers, and read off biomechanics (center of gravity, base of support, balance margin, joint angles, A/B pose comparison). Stack: Vite + Three.js, vanilla JS ES modules, no framework, no tests, no vite config file.

All three mesh layers are imported GLBs whose parts are re-parented onto our own joint rig (`src/skeletonMesh.js`); `main.js` loads them with top-level `await` before building the figures and falls back to procedural geometry on failure. The skeleton and muscle views are anatomical atlases (CC-BY-SA — distributing them requires attribution + a compatible license); the muscle GLB shares the skeleton's atlas frame, so it is loaded only when the skeleton did and bakes with the skeleton's scale. The body view is a pair of clothed Microsoft Rocketbox avatars (`man.glb` / `woman.glb`, MIT) — skinned meshes whose Biped bones are retargeted onto our joints at load. See `public/models/ATTRIBUTION.md` for all licenses.

## Commands

```bash
npm run dev        # Vite dev server at http://localhost:5173
npm run build      # static build to dist/
npm run preview    # serve the build
npm run bake:rig   # (dev server running) freeze the skeleton<->mannequin calibration to src/rigCalibration.js
```

## Architecture

Everything is **data-driven**: each body part, muscle, bone, joint limit, and movement is a row in a table, editable without touching the rest.

- `src/skeletonDef.js` — single source of anatomical truth: joint hierarchy (positions as height fractions, Drillis–Contini), per-axis joint limits in degrees, de Leva mass table (`MASS_SEGMENTS`) used for COG, IK chains, closed-chain anchor map (`ANCHOR_FOR`), dancer-vs-dancer collision capsules (`COLLIDERS`), and `BODY_PARTS`/`PART_OF_NODE` highlight groups. A `BODY_PARTS` row also carries its **highlight `color`** (each part lights in its own hue so several lit at once stay tellable apart — the Highlight panel's swatch overrides it per part, `PART_COLOR` is the default map) and its **`short`/`rep`** pair (the everyday callout name and the joint it hangs on at the Simple label detail: "Foot" on `ankle_L`). **Only the left side is defined; the right side is auto-mirrored.** The sole corner tables (`FOOT_CORNERS_*`/`TOE_CORNERS_*`, the balance footprint) are **sized to the rendered shoe of the man avatar** (measured from the skinned mesh — the rig's own toe segment overshoots the shoe, so the toes joint sits near the shoe TIP and the toe-pad corners only a little past it); a figure whose avatar wears a different shoe scales them per-figure via the `soleScale` constructor option (the follower's shorter heeled shoe uses `front: 0.78`, set where she is constructed in `main.js`).
- `src/skeletonMesh.js` — loads the imported anatomical skeleton GLB (`public/models/skeleton.glb`, CC-BY-SA — see `public/models/ATTRIBUTION.md`) and classifies each of its ~144 named bones onto one of our joint nodes (`classifyBone`). The GLB is Draco-compressed (decoder self-hosted in `public/draco/`) and holds only right-side + axial bones; the left side is mirrored at bake time. Matching is alphanumeric-normalized (GLTFLoader sanitizes names) and side is read from the bone's GLTF group, not the name suffix. Also loads the muscle GLB (`public/models/muscles.glb`, same source/license) via `loadMuscleMeshes`, routing ~68 named main-mover bellies to joint nodes with `classifyMuscle` (an exact normalized-name → `{ node, insert? }` map: `MUSCLE_NODE` gives the bone a belly rides, `MUSCLE_INSERT` names the far joint a bi-articular belly also crosses so it can skin/deform between the two — see `figure.js`); all shipped muscles are right-side and mirror to the left. **The shoulder girdle and rotator cuff ride `scapula`, not `chest`** — every one of them attaches to the blade, and `scapula_L/R` is a real joint four clips drive, so on `chest` the blade slid out from under motionless muscle (measured: 0 mm of motion for all five girdle sheets, 44–83 mm of cuff detachment at ±25°). The sheets insert toward `chest`, the cuff toward `shoulder`, and `GIRDLE_SHEETS` gives the sheets `ride: 'spine'` so they stay in the Torso highlight group rather than following their node into the arm. **Eight LONG TENDONS are shipped** (quadriceps/patellar, calcaneal, biceps + triceps brachii, biceps femoris, semimembranosus, pes anserinus, the long toe extensors) because a belly generally stops short of the joint it acts across — the quadriceps end 5–7 cm above the knee joint line and the calf 13 cm above the ankle — so without them there is nothing spanning the joint and the belly has to be stretched across the gap to reach. A tendon is the part of the unit that really crosses, so shipping it is what lets the belly stay put. **The whole thigh now follows that through, and `MUSCLE_INSERT`'s rule is CONTACT: a belly is skinned across a joint only if its geometry actually TOUCHES the bone on the far side.** Nothing but the four long tendons crosses the knee any more. The quadriceps bellies stop 66–81 mm short of the shin (vastus intermedius 80.9, lateralis 69.9, rectus femoris 69.1, medialis 65.6, against 0.3–0.9 mm for the tendons), so skinned to the tibia regardless, the sliding window parked their far weight band on tissue nowhere near the joint and the knee dragged it across the gap — the group tore to x1.59–1.75 of bind in `kn_flex` and x1.46–1.65 in `hp_flex`, which is the thigh stripped to bare femur with the sartorius flung off it. Freeing that slot is also what fixes the other end: rectus femoris, sartorius, gracilis and the three long hamstrings all REST ON the pelvis (measured gap 1.6–2.4 mm) but had their origins welded to the femur with nothing modelling the hip, so hip flexion carried them bodily off it — sartorius 169 mm, semimembranosus 127, gracilis 101, rectus femoris 77, measured in the pelvis's own frame against 0–9 mm for the correctly-wired hip muscles beside them. They now `insert` toward `pelvis` (all ≤7.5 mm) and their tendons carry the knee; the cost is a 12–38 mm residual on their small shin-side tails in deep knee flexion, which is the same band the pes anserinus tendon itself sits in (32 mm). The three vasti and the short head of biceps femoris touch NEITHER bone, so they are deliberately absent from the table and stay rigid on the femur. A clip whose movers are these bellies must name the tendon in its callout (`QUADS_KNEE`/`HAMSTRINGS_KNEE` in movements.js) or it highlights nothing that can move — and the bellies then read as INERT in `dev-probe-clip-anatomy`, exactly as gastrocnemius already does in the ankle clips. **Gastrocnemius rides `hip` (the femoral condyles) and crosses the KNEE**, with the Achilles carrying its ankle half; on `knee` its femoral origin was welded to the shin and slid 108 mm off the condyles at 145° (80 mm at only 90°, i.e. wrong in every bent-knee tango pose). **The SOLEUS is rigid on `knee` for the matching reason** — it stops 85.3 mm short of the ankle (111.9 mm from the calcaneus), so it fails the CONTACT rule and the Achilles, which reaches the calcaneus at 2.4 mm, does its crossing. Skinned to the ankle it was the visible "calf popping off in plantarflexion" bug; the numbers and the reason its cleanliness on stretch/drift proves nothing are in the `ankle` group of `MUSCLE_INSERT`. Like the quadriceps and the gastrocnemius heads, the soleus now reads INERT in the ankle clips, so `an_pf`/`tg_releve` name the Achilles in its callout (`SOLEUS` in movements.js). **The two BICEPS BRACHII heads are the same rule a third time** — 0.3 mm / 8.0 mm from the humerus but 25.9 mm / 25.7 mm from the forearm, with their geometry stopping 13.8 mm short of the elbow pivot, so the sliding window welded their distal END to a bone they never touch and elbow flexion tore the belly into pieces flung medially over the ribs (worst edge +37.4 mm). They ride the humerus; `Common tendon of biceps brachii` (0.2 mm to the forearm, the only part whose geometry crosses the pivot) does the crossing, and `BICEPS` in movements.js names it. **The TRICEPS by contrast is correctly wired in full** and must stay so: all three heads touch both bones (0.2–5.1 mm each side). With these three fixes the CONTACT audit reads **0 of 59**. The abdominal wall (rectus abdominis + obliques) rides the `pelvis` node (its caudal iliac/pubic attachment) and skins toward the `chest` with a full-length **spread** blend (`TRUNK_SHEETS` in skeletonMesh.js → the `spread` path in figure.js): the belly shears progressively from its pelvis end to its chest end, so a chest-vs-pelvis twist (tango dissociation, which lives almost entirely at the `chest` joint) stretches it on one side and shortens it on the other instead of carrying it along rigidly. It still highlights with the Torso part (`ride: 'spine'`). **`CONTACT_SHEETS` is the third weighting**, beside the default axial split and `TRUNK_SHEETS`' spread: a belly listed there is weighted by which of its two bones the tissue lies on rather than by position along a bone axis — see the `#addSkinnedMuscle` `mode` note under figure.js for why and for the measurements. It holds gluteus maximus, **the whole rotator cuff plus teres major**, latissimus dorsi, and **serratus anterior + pectoralis minor** (blade → RIBS: the axial split along `scapula→chest` handed 74–79% of the serratus tissue lying on a rib to the BLADE, so the digitations rode the blade across the ribs they arise from — 88.6 mm in `sh_abd`, 110.5 in `sc_elev` — now 4–7 mm mean; the cost is the back third of serratus, which is sandwiched between blade and cage, touches both, and is shared. `ride` and `contact` are independent in `classifyMuscle`: these two are girdle sheets AND contact sheets). The cuff is there because the axis, not the table, was wrong: their rows are anatomically right (blade → humerus), but `#addSkinnedMuscle` sizes its window as a fraction of |b − a|, and on `scapula→shoulder` that line runs ACROSS their fibres (teres major 80.0°, subscapularis 80.7°) so each belly projects onto only 34–53 mm of a 165.7 mm inter-node distance and the window swallows it whole — 42% of teres major and 52% of subscapularis ended up committed to neither bone, and `sh_flex` rendered the posed scapula as BARE BONE beside a fully clothed opposite side. This was a regression from the (correct) `chest`→`scapula` move, where the same axis had run down the fibres at 36–54°. By contact, the tissue leaving its bone falls from 228 → 27 mm (teres major), 169 → 54 (subscapularis), 99 → 27 (teres minor), 83 → 48 (infraspinatus), with mis-commitment at 0–16%. **Latissimus dorsi is the third tenant, and the one that needed contact EXTENDED** (`CONTACT_OPTS` in skeletonMesh.js → `contactOpts` on `#addSkinnedMuscle`). Its axial split measured along chest→shoulder, which points UP and out while the tendon runs DOWN the humerus, so the insertion was abandoned progressively down the shaft (committed 0.94 at 50–75 mm below the joint, 0.77 at 75–100, 0.53 at 100–125; 11 of 1054 verts fully on the bone) and at 170° flexion 63% of the tissue touching the humerus left it by >20 mm — a wisp hanging in mid-air behind the arm, and an INERT flag in its own `sh_ir` clip. Two options make contact work for it. `origin: ['chest','spine','pelvis']` pools those bone clouds into nodeA's side of the distance test, because the sheet RIDES `chest` but ARISES from the lumbar spine and iliac crest too (it still FOLLOWS `chest` alone — two-bone skinning has only two frames). `window: [0.35, 0.9]` remaps the ratio so tissue at/below `lo` is wholly the trunk's, and **plain contact without it is WORSE THAN THE BUG**: the lat lies over other muscle, 10–30 mm off bone, the raw ratio reads that stand-off as ~14% humeral, and 14% of a 170° swing drove the sheet forward THROUGH THE RIBCAGE while both end metrics read beautifully. The sweep is recorded beside the table (0.2 lets the back slide 53 mm, 0.5 tears the bridge harder; 0.35 is the loosest that holds the back at 0.2 mm). Result: mis-commitment 43% → 0%, anchor 364–413 → 1–5 mm on both sides across five clips. **Judge any contact retune on the whole SHEET and on screenshots, never on the end metrics alone** — `dev-probe-muscle-anchor.mjs`'s 8 mm contact set cannot see tissue standing off the bone. Not fixed, and not new: in wide abduction the axillary bridge bows ~10 cm lateral of the arm (DQS carries mid-weight tissue round the shoulder on an arc where a taut tendon takes the chord). **`Figure.setMuscleColor`'s highlight grouping is untouched by this** — the cuff is deliberately NOT in `GIRDLE_SHEETS`, so it keeps no `ride` and still highlights with the arm. Also loads the clothed body avatars (`public/models/man.glb` / `woman.glb`, Microsoft Rocketbox, MIT) via `loadBodyMesh`, and exports `BODY_RETARGET` — the table mapping each 3ds Max Biped bone to the joint it snaps to, the bone/joint pair that defines its alignment direction, and its axial-stretch / foot-squash flags (see `Figure.#buildMeshBody`).
- `src/anatomy.js` — **fallback** procedural bone/muscle geometry from data tables, used only if a GLB fails to load: `LONG` plus small builders (skull, ribcage, pelvis, spine, hand, foot), and the muscle spindles (`MUSCLES_LEFT` / `MUSCLES_CENTER`).
- `scripts/build-muscles.mjs` — one-off asset pipeline (gltf-transform, run manually): trims the AnatomyTOOL "Upper limb" / "Lower limb" / "Muscles of thorax, abdomen and back" source GLBs down to the classified main-mover muscles, flattens materials, merges all three (deduping bellies that appear in more than one source, so the trunk model contributes only the abdominal wall), and Draco-compresses to `public/models/muscles.glb`. Imports `classifyMuscle` so its keep-list can't drift from the runtime.
- `scripts/build-body.mjs` — one-off asset pipeline for the body avatars: takes an FBX2glTF conversion of a Microsoft Rocketbox avatar (FBX → GLB comes out untextured because the FBX references 3ds Max map names), decodes the source TGA textures itself, downsizes them to 1024², wires them onto the body/head/opacity materials, and Draco-compresses to `public/models/man.glb` / `woman.glb`. The skin (bones + weights) passes through intact.
- `src/figure.js` — assembles a dancer as an Object3D joint tree (not a skinned mesh) with three toggleable mesh layers (skeleton / body / muscles); `addMesh(node, mesh, layer)` is the attach point for any geometry. `#buildMeshSkeleton()` scales the atlas bones to the figure's height, bakes each into its joint node's local frame (mirroring right→left, reversing winding), and merges per node+material into one mesh to keep draw calls low. **Limb bones (and their muscles) ride a parallel display-only "atlas" sub-tree, not the rig nodes.** The rig's anthropometric joint nodes (Drillis–Contini, for correct COG) sit a few cm off the imported skeleton's own joint centers, so a bone baked rigidly on a rig node pivots about the wrong point and swings visibly apart from its neighbour as a joint bends (a deeply-bent elbow opened a ~5 cm gap). `#buildAtlasNodes()` builds `this.atlasNodes` — an Object3D per seated limb joint, placed at the atlas joint center (`calibration.rest`) and slaved to the rig joint's local rotation by `syncAtlasNodes()` (called from every pose-mutating path: `setPose`/`resetPose`/`setJointDegrees`/`app.editJoint`, plus `main.js`'s loop) — so bones hung there pivot about the anatomical joint and stay welded in any pose. `#seatNode(name)` returns the atlas node if seated, else the rig node; the bone/muscle bakes and `#alignEndpointGeometry` all resolve through it. A whole limb chain must be seated together (a bone welds to its neighbour only if both ride matching trees); **all four limb chains are seated**: the arms (scapula → shoulder → elbow → wrist, the scapula staying a rig node as the chain anchor) and the legs (hip → knee → ankle → toes, the pelvis as the rig anchor). A heeled figure's atlas ankle/toes seats are raised by its `heelLift` (mirroring the rig ankle lift) so they sit exactly where the retargeted shoe's foot bones snap, and `#applyHeel` resolves its pitch target and the foot meshes' parent origins through `#seatNode`. The atlas fans the hand's fingers ~1.7× wider than the clothed avatar carries them, so `#handDesplayRotations()` adducts index/ring/pinky toward the middle finger at bake time (fraction `HAND_DESPLAY`, tuned to the avatar's own spread) so the bare skeletal fingers sit inside the glove instead of overshooting it. `#buildMeshMuscles()` does the same bake/mirror for the muscle GLB using the *skeleton's* atlas scale, keeping muscles as individual named meshes (each carries `userData.muscleName`). A belly that only rides one bone is parented rigidly to its joint; a bi-articular belly (one with an `insert`, e.g. biceps, gastrocnemius, rectus femoris) is baked in figure-local space, hung off `group`, and **skinned between its two joints** — `#addSkinnedMuscle` computes a per-vertex weight along the origin→insertion axis, and `updateMuscleSkin()` re-blends the baked positions/normals from the two joints' current transforms every frame. **The transition window is the load-bearing part, and it must be allowed to SLIDE.** It belongs at the joint — flesh is rigid on its bone and only the crossing tendon deforms — but a belly that does not straddle the pivot has every vertex on one side of the ramp and so never commits to the bone across the joint. Narrowing cannot fix that (a band centred on the pivot has nothing to narrow *toward*), which is why 44 of 106 bellies used to never saturate and the whole quadriceps group and triceps surae sat at weight **0**, welded to the wrong bone through a 145° bend, while the adductors (the same failure mirrored, starting *below* the hip) capped at 0.28. So the window slides onto the geometry when `hasDistal`/`hasProx` say the belly misses the pivot, and is also capped at half the belly's own span, or parked against one end it runs off the other and that end never saturates either. `REACH` (2% of the bone) is what stops a 1.7 mm sliver counting as an attachment — pectineus's did, collapsing its band to a 1.4 mm knife edge whose weights still *read* a healthy 0..1. **Two things here were tried and are worse — do not "unify" them.** The two branches measure along different axes on purpose: for a belly riding nodeA's bone the a→b line IS that bone, and switching it to the crossed joint's distal bone breaks every trunk→limb muscle, because a hanging limb runs parallel to the torso (measured along the humerus, latissimus's lumbar origin projects further "distal" than its own humeral insertion, so 71% of the sheet welded itself to the arm). And a negative `sMax` means the belly stops short of the pivot, not that the axis is wrong. `#distalBoneDir` must also skip a node's own pick sphere and the parallel atlas node of a seated joint: both carry a `jointName`, and averaging them in halved every unseated bone's length (a 303 mm humerus read 151 mm), which fed straight into the band width (called from `main.js`'s loop and from `setPose`; it no-ops unless the muscle layer is visible). **The axial split is not the only weighting: `#addSkinnedMuscle` takes a `mode`** — `null` (the split above, nearly every belly), `'spread'` (the abdominal wall's full-length ramp) or `'contact'` (`#contactWeights`: each vertex is shared between the two bones by how close it lies to each, `dA / (dA + dB)` smoothstepped, measured against `#boneCloud`'s ~3000-point sub-sample of the bone surface hanging on each node). Contact needs no axis, and that is its whole reason to exist: **gluteus maximus** is a fan from the sacrum, coccyx and posterior ilium out to the femur, and HALF ITS ORIGIN LIES BELOW THE HIP JOINT'S HEIGHT, so an axis along the femur reads that sacral tissue as "distal" and welds it to the thigh — through hip flexion the tissue actually touching the pelvis drifted 55 mm on average and 130 mm at worst off the bone. By contact it holds to 1.1 mm (3.5 max) with the insertion at 2.0 mm (5.6 max, from 23.1) and no more stretch than before. Medius and minimus stay on the axial split, which suits fibres that run down the limb (their origins already read 0 mm). Which bellies use it is `CONTACT_SHEETS` in skeletonMesh.js, where the two alternatives that were measured and are worse are recorded. What contact does NOT fix: at 120° the belly still slings a few cm under the ischium, because blending two rigid frames carries mid-belly tissue round the hip at its resting radius where a real gluteus maximus is pulled taut over the ischial tuberosity — that needs a wrapping surface, which two-bone skinning cannot express. This is CPU skinning, not `THREE.SkinnedMesh`, so the muscle's own frame never moves and the skin stays correct as the whole dancer translates/turns. `#buildMeshBody()` attaches the clothed avatar: it keeps the GLB's `THREE.SkinnedMesh` + skin weights but re-parents each Biped bone onto the matching joint node with a constant local matrix — onto the **atlas** node (`#seatNode`) for seated limb joints, so the clothed arm pivots about the same anatomical joint as the skeleton/muscle layers and stays welded to them through any bend (a rig-node pivot fanned the layers apart — a bent arm split the skeleton ~18 cm off the sleeve), and onto the rig node for everything unseated (torso/legs). To make the three layers coincide (so they overlay when toggled together), the body is retargeted onto the *atlas* rest pose, not the rig's own rest — the skeleton and muscle layers render the atlas pose (arms slightly abducted, hands/feet splayed), so `#atlasLimbRest()` estimates each limb joint's atlas position from the loaded skeleton bones (the meeting point of adjacent bone clusters). This estimate is computed once per build into `this.calibration` — the **single neutral rest** the body retarget and the endpoint roll both consume, so all layers reference one meeting point instead of re-deriving it inline. Each Biped bone snaps its origin to that atlas joint, aligns its bind direction along the atlas segment, and stretches axially to reach it (torso joints, which already agree, stay on the rig rest). A world-Y squash on foot/toe bones keeps shoe soles — heels included — grazing y = 0 at rest. Unmapped bones (fingers, face, clavicles) ride their re-parented ancestor unchanged, and the mesh hangs off `group` in 'attached' bind mode so all deformation is GPU-skinned with no per-frame CPU cost. `#alignEndpointGeometry()` then rolls the skeletal hands onto the clothed hands' orientation (the atlas holds the hand rolled differently from the avatar's). `#endpointAlignR` fits the *optimal* rotation (`hornRotation`, Horn's quaternion method) that overlays six corresponding regions at once — the five finger clusters and the palm block — using atlas-bone centroids for the skeleton and dominant-Biped-bone skin centroids for the body; the palm is weighted up since the two hands are shaped differently and no rigid rotation makes them fully coincide (frame-from-axes constructions fail here because the opposable thumb sits off the palm plane and tilts any single normal differently in each layer). Orientation (`fingers` along the middle finger's proximal phalanx, `palm` = the direction a curl first sweeps the fingertips, plus the `offset` quaternion from the rig-canonical hand frame) is measured in the **rig wrist's** frame, which is valid because the rig and atlas wrists share their local rotations and so agree in orientation exactly (measured: 0.0° apart in every pose); embrace.js conjugates its clasp/pronation targets by it, and `palmDirWorld(side)` / `fingerDirWorld(side)` expose the visible palm normal and finger aim. **Position is a different frame and a different story**: `center` (the palm's middle, midway between the avatar's own hand-bone origin and its knuckle row) is stored in the **atlas** wrist's frame via `#seatNode`, because that is the node the clothed hand is welded to, and `palmPosWorld(side)` reads it back. The two wrists share rotations but NOT positions, so they drift apart as the arm flexes — 6 cm with the arm hanging, **~18 cm at the embrace's -120° elbow**. Anything that POSITIONS a hand off `wrist_*`/`hand_*` is therefore aiming a phantom: it is what let the embrace solve a flawless palm-to-palm clasp on the rig nodes while the rendered hands hung ~15 cm apart in mid-air, invisible to a whole verification suite that also only ever measured rig nodes. A side bend rendered as a straight lumbar stick hinged on the sacrum with a second break at L1/T12, and T10–T12, which sit BELOW the chest pivot, swung the wrong way out from under L1. `SpineColumn` (built in `#build` right after the skeleton bake, at rest; `update()` is called from `syncAtlasNodes` and `setLayers`) gives vertebra k the orientation `q_spine^A(k) · q_chest^B(k)`, A and B being LINEAR ramps in rest height (constant curvature = evenly distributed), chains the positions disc to disc from the sacrum, and CPU-skins the `spine`/`chest` node meshes in their own node frames (the geometry attribute stays the truth, so labels, landmarks, picking, `#boneCloud` and the probes read it as before; bounds are inflated by `BOUNDS_SLACK` because culling and the raycaster's early-out read the REST bounds). **No rig node moves, nothing changes at rest (so no re-bake), and both ends are exact**: the sacrum stays in the pelvis frame and T1 lands exactly on the rigid chest frame, so the neck, the clavicles and the sternum stay welded. **Each ramp is SYMMETRIC ABOUT ITS RIG PIVOT, and that is the load-bearing choice** — the far end's displacement is set by the first moment of the rotation density, so a hinge at p and an even bend over [p − a, p + a] carry T1 to the same place to second order. Spreading each joint over its ANATOMICAL region instead (lumbar = L5–L1, thoracic = T12–T1) puts the chest bend's centroid 6 cm above the chest pivot and leaves T1 46 mm short of the shoulders at 25°. So the chest ramp runs from T1 down to ~L3 (0.598–0.842H) and OVERLAPS the lumbar one (0.567–0.673H); that is the honest drawing of a rig whose "thoracic" hinge sits at T9/T10, and the per-disc result is anatomical anyway (side bend 20° + 25°: lumbar discs 3–6° each, thoracic 1–2°). - **`#atlasLimbRest` locates the shoulder and hip from the CHILD side**, not the parent. Its general rule — "the verts of this bone nearest the parent cluster's centroid" — is sound while the parent is a limb bone (the humerus's centroid sits up the arm, so the nearest radius/ulna verts really are the elbow), but it fails badly where the parent is a torso cluster: the chest's and pelvis's centroids lie far away and medial and drag the estimate down and inward. - `src/landmarks.js` — **named anatomical points, located independently in each layer.** A landmark carries a separate recipe per layer (a bone-name regex plus a reducer: centroid, extreme along a figure-local axis, or nearest/farthest from a joint), so "the layers agree" becomes a real measurement. This is deliberately NOT what `dev-verify-alignment.mjs` measures: that compares the centroid of everything hanging on a joint node against the centroid of the skin attributed to it, and those are different objects (the skeleton's `spine` node owns the whole vertebral column and ribcage; the avatar's Spine bone owns a patch of skin), so its ~150 mm torso numbers are largely ownership artifact rather than misalignment. **Torso landmarks must be palpable** — acromion, iliac crest, C7 spinous, jugular notch — because elsewhere in the trunk bone sits deep to skin and a large gap is anatomy; limb landmarks are mostly endpoints for the same reason. A landmark's vertex SET is frozen at bind (rest) so it stays the same material point; its POSITION is read live, so it resolves in any pose. Two traps encoded here: `getVertexPosition` on a SkinnedMesh in 'attached' bind mode returns **figure-local, not world** (the model matrix is applied at render time — `dev-verify-alignment.mjs` reads it raw and is only correct because it zeroes `group.position` first), and an axis-based pick on a *sided* landmark must mirror x for the right side or the two sides silently select opposite edges of the same bone (measured: a 300 mm acromion gap that was pure recipe). Bone-level selection against the merged skeleton meshes works because `#buildMeshSkeleton` records `mesh.userData.boneRanges`. Also owns **`ENDPOINT_FITS`** — the corresponding REGIONS that `Figure.#endpointAlignR` runs its weighted similarity fit (Umeyama/Horn) over, so the fit is data like everything else and shares the landmark recipe language (a region is just a landmark that keeps many verts instead of a few). A region is only VALID if its two recipes describe the same sub-object, so the centroids are concentric; and the regions must not be collinear, or the roll about that line is unconstrained. Hand regions are `raw: true` (sampled from the un-baked atlas bones, `perBone` verts each) — **this deliberately preserves the frozen calibration**: reading the rendered geometry instead is arguably more correct, since the rendered hand carries the `HAND_DESPLAY` finger tuck, but switching rotated the frozen hand roll 9° and moved `palm_center` 4→8 mm, so it is left as a separately testable question rather than a refactor side effect. **The foot uses `mode: 'axis'`, not a similarity fit** — what matters for a foot is that it POINTS the way the shoe does, so the fit aligns the MIDLINE (dominant principal axis) of the skeletal foot with the shoe's and does nothing else. The fit is: stretch along the midline to match LENGTH (one axis only), rotate the midline onto the body's, and place the two midlines' **segment midpoints** together. Measured: the skeletal foot was **18.8° off** (leader) / **16.7°** (follower) → now 0.1° / 1.7°; foot `tipGap` 42.7 → 22–26 mm; skeletal foot reach now matches the shoe's (leader 173 vs 177, follower 134 vs 136, from 159/177 and 157/136). Three failed approaches are recorded in the table comment so they are not retried. (1) A hand-style *similarity* fit made it worse (`heel_back` 21→55 mm): a glove follows a hand so their regions are concentric, but a SHOE does not follow a foot (heel block, toe box), and a UNIFORM scale distorts width to fix length — the feet differ in length far more than width. (2) Aligning the two CENTROIDS slid the foot down its own length until its farthest-from-ankle point flipped from toe to heel — 290 mm of error, **while `measureAxes` reported a perfect 0.1°**, because a metric that measures what the fit forces is circular; the independent check that caught it is `dev-verify-alignment.mjs`'s foot `tipGap`. (3) Centroids still failed *after* length-matching (312 mm): a centroid is a mass average and a bone cloud bunches differently from the shoe skin around it, so the two never correspond. The **segment midpoint** is purely geometric, so with equal extents matching midpoints also matches both ENDS. On a heeled figure `heel_back` legitimately reads ~48 mm (calcaneus vs the shoe's heel BLOCK, which hangs below and behind it) against ~11 mm for the leader's flat shoe — it is informational, not gated; aim and `toe_tip` are the foot's real tests. The axis fit aligns the skeletal foot to the shoe's flat OUTER sole (0.1°/1.7° after the fit), which for a block heel leaves the bones lying FLAT — not standing inside the heel. So `#applyHeel`/`#pitchHeeledFoot` then pitches the foot heel-up by the heel angle (composed with the fit matrix, never overwriting it — see figure.js), which is a deliberate SAGITTAL tilt that moves the foot's midline ~11° off the shoe's flat sole. `measureAxes` reports the whole relative orientation as an **undirected** line angle (folding the occasional ~169° forward-flip back to ~11°); it is pose-invariant (the foot and shoe rotate together), so it equals ~0 for a flat foot and the heel pitch for a heeled one, and `dev-verify-frames.mjs` allows `AXIS_DEG_TOL + Figure.heelPitchDeg` for the foot-aim check.

- `src/embrace.js` — the tango embrace as per-frame constraints (re-applied from `main.js`'s loop; UI "Embrace" section → `app.setEmbrace`), modelled on the real embrace. "Hold embrace (arms)", open side: the leader's LEFT hand holds the follower's RIGHT palm to palm around a clasp point stored in both chest frames (live target = their midpoint, so it follows the couple; engaging the checkbox seeds it at the canonical hold — out past the open-side shoulders, midway between the chests, at the "Clasp height" (`app.setClaspHeight` → `Embrace.setClaspHeight`; default shoulder level, a natural close-embrace frame with the elbows hanging, the slider raises it toward a salon frame) — the horizontal placement chosen so the clasp orientation is anatomically reachable, and dragging an open-side hand re-captures it. The elbow height follows the clasp height, not the finger tilt: the hand sits above the shoulder only when the clasp is raised, and that is what lifts the elbow. The palm surfaces touch (`PALM_GAP` between centers) **along the couple axis** (leader chest → follower chest, horizontal): his palm faces her, hers faces him — both medial facings a real wrist can deliver; a normal that asks a close-in palm to face laterally away from its own chest lands in the pronation dead zone at every swivel and the hand turns its back on the partner (the original clasp bug). All hand-orientation targets (clasp and closed-side `#pronate`) are expressed in the figure's **mesh-truth hand frame** (`Figure.handMesh`), not the rig's canonical wrist axes: the Biped bind carries each avatar palm rolled ~110° about the forearm from the rig's palms-forward wrist-local +Z, so aiming the node axis at the partner showed them the back of the hand (the leader clasp flip) while the node-frame numbers read perfect; `Figure.palmDirWorld(side)` is the visible palm normal dev-verify-embrace checks. Hand **positions** are mesh truth for the same reason: `handCenter(figure, side)` is the single choke point and it returns `Figure.palmPosWorld` (the visible palm center), never the rig wrist→hand midpoint — the clothed hand rides the atlas wrist, which sits ~18 cm from the rig wrist at the embrace's own deep elbow flexion, so solving on rig nodes joined two phantom hands perfectly while the rendered ones hung ~15 cm apart. `handCenter` calls `syncAtlasNodes()` before reading, and must: the IK inside `#solveArm` writes rig rotations directly without one, so an unsynced read returns the palm for the previous iteration's pose and turns the solve's fixed-point correction into a divergent feedback loop (measured: the hands flew 122 cm apart). `#solveArm` seeds and re-aims its wrist target with the **currently measured** rig-wrist→palm offset each pass — that offset is large and pose-dependent, not the old half-a-hand-along-the-fingers guess. Both joined hands aim for a vertical-handshake finger direction: fingers up, tilted from vertical by the "Clasp tilt" UI slider (`app.setClaspTilt` → `Embrace.setTilt`) toward the couple's midline, plus a soft `CLASP_WRAP_DEG` lean of each hand's fingers over the back of the partner's (at the default shoulder-height clasp the leader's open hand is limit-bound and leans up-and-in rather than straight up — the tilt has full authority only in the raised salon frame), and the clothed avatars' finger bones curl lightly (`Figure.setFingerCurl` at `CLASP_CURL`, tables in figure.js). The clasp hand orientation is solved exactly: fingers + palm pin the whole hand frame, decomposed to elbow pronation + wrist flex/deviation as YXZ Euler (both branches tried), the arm's swivel is chosen by sampling the elbow's circle (`#swivelForPalm`), and when the geometry is strained the fingers lean over in the palm plane (`#LEANS`) — candidates are ranked by the **achieved** palm/finger error after limit-clamping (palm weighted double), never by raw limit violation. Closed side: BOTH palms rest on the partner's back — his right on her back below her left shoulder blade (his arm passing UNDER her left arm), and her left high on his back behind his right shoulder blade, her arm draped OVER his and around his shoulder (`CLOSED_TARGETS`, fractions in the partner's chest frame). Hers is not a fixed point: it lerps out to `FOLLOWER_OPEN_REST` (his deltoid) by `openness()` as the chests separate from close-embrace to salon distance, because reaching AROUND him costs far more arm than resting on the near side of him and her arm is the shorter of the two — at salon distance the back point sits ~3 cm past her fingertips, and an out-of-reach embrace goal does not degrade into a shortfall, it ratchets the shoulder into its `-170°/-30°` limit corner and stands the arm straight up over her head (measured: palm 45 cm off, 38 cm too high). `MAX_REACH` in `#reachClamp` is the backstop for the same failure — the far-end twin of the long-standing `MIN_REACH` clamp, since both ends are the same rule (a goal the arm cannot occupy makes the per-frame solve ratchet instead of settle). Both closed-side hands `#pronate` toward the PARTNER'S CHEST NODE — the torso axis is square to the back surface wherever on it the hand landed; aiming hers at the midpoint of his upper arm (which is roughly where her hand already was) made the direction near-degenerate and left her palm 82° off the surface it was supposed to be lying on. All four arms re-solve every frame via `solveTwoBone`; closed-side arms keep their authored swivel (`embraceArms` pose in presets.js gives the elbows-down hang and the over/under layering) — don't force an elbow roll there, it fights joint limits on these close-in reaches and destabilizes the per-frame solve. "Close embrace": holds the horizontal chest-to-chest distance at `CONTACT_FRACTION`·(H₁+H₂) by sliding a dancer; distance-only, so the contact point can rotate around either torso (giros, calesita). Both constraints skip whatever the user is editing (`embraceEditing()` in main.js) and move the partner instead; editing an open-side arm re-captures the clasp so the partner tracks the user's hand. The closed side only solves while the couple roughly face each other (`#facing()`) — otherwise those rest points sit behind the shoulder's range and the per-frame solve would ratchet the arms into overhead poses (same reason `#solveArm` clamps its goal to `MIN_REACH` from the shoulder; `closedGoalWorld` exposes the clamped rest point, which in close embrace is where the follower's palm actually lands on his deltoid).
- `src/pins.js` — **contact pins**: user-authored per-frame constraints holding a spot on one dancer against a spot on the other (custom holds, a parada's foot contact). Each spot is stored in the local frame of the joint node nearest the click (`nearestJointNode`), so it rides that body part. The partner of whoever is being edited adapts (embrace convention; default the follower), and HOW depends on where the adapting end rides: an arm spot re-solves that arm via two-bone IK, a leg spot re-solves the leg (then re-clamps the mover to the floor), a torso/head spot slides the whole dancer horizontally. `maintainBody` (translation pins) runs with the embrace torso pull — before collision and the floor clamp, which push back; `maintainLimbs` runs after `maintainHands`, so a pin on an embrace arm deliberately overrides the embrace for that arm. Authoring: the topbar "Pin spots" mode (`app.pinClick` — first click sets a pending spot, a click on the other dancer completes the pin); `app.addPin` is the scriptable path; the sidebar "Contact pins" section lists/releases them. End markers turn amber with a strain line when anatomy can't close the gap; spots placed inside the partner's collision volume legitimately rest at surface contact (collision wins — do not "fix" this).
- `src/collision.js` — dancer-vs-dancer body collision as a per-frame constraint (runs in `main.js`'s loop between the torso-contact pull and the floor clamp): each dancer is the `COLLIDERS` capsule set from skeletonDef.js — the body `core` (torso/head/legs/feet, `group:'core'`) plus thin `arm` capsules (upper arm + forearm, no hand, `group:'arm'`). Any penetration deeper than ~2 mm is resolved by sliding the partner of whoever is being edited (default: the follower yields, same convention as the embrace) horizontally along the deepest contact's normal. The slide length is found by bracketing + bisecting the whole capsule-set clearance — interleaved limbs (crossed shins, a foot between the partner's feet) make clearance along a slide non-monotone, so fixed-step pushing can oscillate and never settle; the bisection lands the surfaces just touching (±0.5 mm), so contact is preserved, never repelled. **Arm capsules are colliders ONLY for the one arm the user is actively posing** (`resolveBodyCollision`'s `editedArm` arg, computed in main.js from the edited joint), and only against the partner's core — so dragging an arm into the partner can't pass through them, it displaces them, exactly like the body core does; the resting/wrapping embrace arms are deliberately NOT colliders (they lie ON and AROUND the partner by design — colliding them all the time shoves the couple apart and wrecks the authored presets), and arm↔arm never collides (the closed-side arms cross by design). The hand is never a collider (it is what the embrace rests on the partner). The trunk radii sit just inside the close-embrace `CONTACT_FRACTION` so held torso contact rests at exact surface touch without the two constraints fighting; head/shoulder radii are calibrated so the shipped contact presets (close embrace, walk) reach the held distance with ~0 clearance. `bodyClearance`/`bodyContacts` (also `app.bodyClearance()`) report the tightest **body-core** pairs (arms excluded — an arm resting on the body is contact, not penetration) for verification and radius tuning.
- `src/analysis.js` — COG from segment masses, foot contacts, convex hull, balance margin, key angles; weight distribution between feet (`weightDistribution`: L/R split, support foot, heel/mid/ball, on-axis) and tango metrics (`tangoStats`: dissociation, step length, turnout). - `src/presets.js` — starting poses. **Joint sign conventions are documented at the top of this file** — read them before authoring poses. Two things about the embrace presets are load-bearing and easy to undo by accident. (1) **An embrace preset must be authored AT the embrace's own contact distance and with the real tango lateral OFFSET** (Close embrace: chests 21.3 cm apart, follower ~14 cm to the leader's right). What decides whether the two heads pass cheek to cheek or meet head-on is that offset, NOT the neck yaw — a head capsule runs `head→headTop`, i.e. nearly along the yaw axis, so yawing barely moves it. Measured at contact distance, the head capsules overlap **7.9 cm at a 5 cm offset** and 3.1 cm at 11 cm; only ~14 cm clears them. Author it square and torso contact is not merely unreached but geometrically impossible, and the failure is not a gap — the close-embrace pull hauls, collision refuses, and the leftover tangential push **walks the follower around the leader** until the heads clear, which they only do once she is ~22 cm round on his LEFT, the wrong side of the embrace, settling 4 cm short of contact. That happened on every engage. Authored at the offset the pose is already the solution and engaging the constraints moves nobody. The couple settles at lat ≈ −14 cm from any authored offset between −11 and −14, which is the check that ~14 is the geometry's own equilibrium and not a tuned number. (2) **`embraceArms`'s arm angles are BAKED from the embrace solve, not hand-authored** — apply the preset, tick both constraints, let it settle, dump the eight arm joints. Hand-authored angles went stale invisibly when the arm chain moved onto the anatomical joint centres, because the constraints overwrite the arms as soon as they are engaged; with them OFF, which is how the app STARTS, the stale angles were what the user saw — both dancers with arms flung out, clasp hands 32 cm apart, the leader's right palm 48 cm in front of his own chest instead of around her back. - `src/labels.js` — **anatomy labels**: named callouts on bones, muscles and joints (topbar **Label** mode; sidebar "Labels" section; `app.addLabel` / `labelHighlighted` / `clearLabels` script it). Laid out as textbook **margin callouts** — a column of text either side of the figure with a leader line to each structure — because that is the one layout that never overlaps itself or the anatomy. A label's anchor is a material point: a joint's `surfacePos`, or **one vertex** of its bone/muscle mesh read live, so it rides any pose including the CPU-skinned bi-articular bellies (a clicked label anchors at the clicked vertex — visible from that view by construction; an auto label takes the vertex on the camera-facing side of the structure's middle). Bones are addressable inside the merged skeleton meshes through `userData.boneRanges`, which now also carries each range's `side` ('R' source / 'L' mirrored copy / null axial) — that flag is how `boneLabel` knows whether a trailing `r` is the atlas's merged side tag ("Femurr") or part of the name ("Vomer"). "Label highlighted" names everything the Highlight / Muscles panels pick out, with `AUTO_GROUPS` collapsing small bones into one callout ("Ribs", "Carpal bones") and `AUTO_SKIP` dropping teeth etc., so a whole torso yields ~35 readable callouts rather than 150. **Two detail levels** (`labels.detail`, `app.setLabelDetail`, the Labels section's Simple/Detailed toggle) — Simple is a different slide, not a shorter one: a click anywhere on a structure names the BODY PART it belongs to (`BODY_PARTS.short`, anchored on that part's `rep` joint — clicking the femur gives one "Leg"), a joint reads its everyday word (`JOINT_LABELS_SIMPLE`: "Knee", not "Knee joint"), and "Label highlighted" yields ONE callout per lit part in any layer (9 parts → 9 callouts vs ~35 bones). Muscles lit by hand in the Muscles panel keep their own names at either level — they have no plainer one. Existing callouts keep the text they were given, so the toggle never rewrites a slide you have already edited. Joint callouts (and therefore every Simple one) draw in blue — `KIND_COLORS`. **A callout's accent is `accentColor(label)`, not the kind map directly**: a muscle given its own colour in the view (`Figure.setMuscleColor`) carries that colour into its pill's edge bar and its anchor dot, so the belly and the label naming it read as one thing on a slide. A callout can also carry a colour of its OWN (`setColor` → `label.color`, serialized with it), which beats both — that is the path for a bone or a joint, which has no belly to hold one. - `src/movements.js` — **the movement-clip library, one row per movement** (47: the standard single-joint ROM set + trunk/pelvis/neck + tango dissociation, hip pivot, relevé). - `src/studio.js` — **the presentation studio**: the label/overlay canvas, backdrop, the 16:9 slide frame, photo + video export, and the clip player. **The overlay is a second 2D canvas stacked on the WebGL one**, not sprites and not a texture: labels, the clip title, the angle arc and the captions are screen-space, so they are drawn with Canvas 2D, and an export REDRAWS them at the export resolution onto a composite canvas (crisp at 1080p or 4K, never a scaled screenshot). **Every overlay size is a fraction of the frame height**, so the live view, a 1080p clip and a 4K photo show the same picture. Consequence: the GL canvas alone no longer holds the whole picture — anything that exports must go through `studio.photoDataURL` / `studio.startRecorder` (`app.photoDataURL` and `app.recordPlayback` do). The **slide frame** letterboxes the view beside the sidebar and renders at exactly 1920×1080 (pixelRatio 1 + explicit CSS size — three's `setSize` floors width×ratio, and a fractional ratio lands on 1919 px, an odd width H.264 refuses). **A sequence keyframe's `kf.caption` is drawn on the overlay too** (`drawCaption`), in the backdrop's ink, in a rounded band along the **BOTTOM** of the frame: the clip title hangs over the top of the shot's own points (and is draggable, which `titleBottom` tracks) and the callout columns own both margins, so the bottom strip is the one region nothing else claims — and it is where a viewer expects a subtitle. Its sizes are fractions of the frame height like every other overlay element (`CAPTION_FONT` 0.034, under the title's 0.052), it wraps to three lines then ellipsizes, and it reserves its height back through `Labels.layout`'s `bottom` option so callouts are pushed UP rather than written over. **A sequence keyframe also draws its own NAME** (`drawSeqName`, `studio.setSeqName`), beside the caption's band: the caption says a SENTENCE to the class at the foot of the frame, the name marks the STEP, so it reads as a heading and defaults to the frame's **top-left** — the clip title centres itself over the dancer, the callout columns own both margins from their first row down and the caption owns the foot, so that corner is the one region unclaimed. **The default clears the topbar** (`topbarOverlap`, measured like `sidebarWidth`, 0 in Present mode): in the window frame the canvas runs up BEHIND the toolbar, and a name pinned at 0.035h was drawn under the buttons — half hidden and unreachable by the very drag meant to move it; an export applies the same clearance, as `right` does for the sidebar, so the photo matches the live view, and a dragged placement is left exactly where the user put it. It reserves callout `top` only while it is in the frame's top band (composed with the title's by `Math.max`), and **the caption now reserves the bottom strip ONLY while its foot is still down there** — the mirror of `titleBottom`'s rule. **All three blocks answer ONE drag routine** (`BLOCKS`/`blockHit`/`beginBlockDrag`/`dragBlockTo`/`endBlockDrag`/`blockPos`/`setBlockPos`, replacing the title-only `titleHit`/`beginTitleDrag`/… family); a block's box is recorded on the LIVE pass only and is null whenever nothing is drawn, so **a hidden caption or an unnamed keyframe is inert with no flag of its own**, and `drawOverlay` clears `titleBox` when no clip runs so a stale box cannot leave a phantom the pointer can still grab. `pos.x` is the block's CENTRE and `pos.y` its TOP for every block. `setCaption(text, color)` / `setSeqName(text, color)` take the keyframe's own ink (null = the backdrop's); the pill and its edge stay the theme's. A second tap on either block opens the colour picker for THAT keyframe (`blockTap` in main.js, counted in `pointerup`). **Backdrops**: studio floor / clean dark / clean white / transparent (the renderer is `alpha: true`; PNG photos only — a video falls back to dark); off the studio floor the wood is swapped for a `ShadowMaterial` so the dancer still stands on something. **Clips** (`enterClip`/`playClip`/`scrubClip`/`recordClip`/`exitClip`): one dancer alone, partner hidden, **keeping the pose and placement they were already in** — a teacher showing what hip flexion looks like inside a cruzada wants the pose they built, not a dancer who snaps to a T-pose as the clip opens. `studio.clipOptions.anatomical` (default **false**), the `anatomical` option on `enterClip`, and `studio.clipAnatomical()` / the sidebar's **⟲ Anatomical position** button apply the textbook `NEUTRAL` stance at the origin instead. Keeping the pose is safe because nothing downstream assumes neutral: `drive` targets are absolute angles and the angle readout measures the marker's swing since the pose the clip OPENED on (`clip.baseAngles`), so it reports the movement actually made from here. **What is SHOWN is that measurement quoted at the textbook's granularity** (`romStep`/`romEnd`/`romShow`): 10° steps for an arc of 30° or more, 5° below that, and the hold always reads the range ROUNDED UP, so hip flexion's measured 117° out of a real tango pose reads the 120° the MOVEMENTS table advertises. Rounding up forgives half a degree (`ROM_EPS`) or a movement that measures a hair over a round number — elbow flexion comes back as exactly 70.0000… — gets quoted a whole step too high. Only the display rounds: `clip.motion.end`/`.angle` stay the raw radians, which is what `dev-verify-studio.mjs` gates on. A row's own `base` test position (elbow at 90° for shoulder rotation) is applied either way — it is part of the movement's definition and the marker, arc and plane are defined against it. **`dev-verify-studio.mjs` must pass `anatomical: true`** for its direction checks: every range and direction in `MOVEMENTS` is DEFINED from the anatomical position, and measured from an embrace instead the starting pose pollutes the direction and correct rows read as moving the wrong way (measured: 17 of 94 checks). The couple constraints are held off, the couple constraints held off (`held = app.anchored || studio.clipActive` in main.js — the floor clamp still runs, which is what turns the relevé row's plain FK into a rise), balance visuals and pick spheres out of shot; `exitClip` restores the couple state, camera, frame and visibility exactly. The motion frame is geometric, not tabulated: the world axis comes from three's XYZ Euler order (a change in x turns about the PARENT's x, y about Rx·ŷ, z about the node's own z — exact even when the test position already bent another axis), the angle is the marker's swing about that axis since neutral (unwrapped, so a 179° abduction doesn't flip at ±180), and the end range is measured by stepping the pose out, not read off the row. **That frame is held in the REFERENCE SEGMENT's coordinates, not in world** (`refNode` + `m.axisRef`/`m.u0Ref`, rebuilt by `measure` every tick): the reference is the nearest ancestor of the primary joint the clip does NOT drive — the thigh for knee extension — so the axis, the zero direction and the arc drawn on them turn WITH the limb when the user goes on posing the dancer, which they now do, because the clip keeps the pose it opened in. Frozen in world, every degree of a hip swing was booked as knee movement and then subtracted from the range: carrying the hip back 75° to show knee extension out of a real tango position read **70° before the knee had moved at all**, and the knee's full 100° extension then quoted 30°. The exclusion of driven joints is the load-bearing half — for `sh_abd` the scapula IS driven (the scapulohumeral rhythm splits one swing between two joints), so the frame climbs past it to the chest or the girdle's share would be cancelled out of the movement it belongs to. `axisFig` rows keep the FIGURE as their axis frame by definition (a relevé turns about the ball of the foot). During a clip the reference node is by construction not driven, so this reproduces the old frozen frame exactly — which is why `dev-verify-studio.mjs` (which measures every row from the anatomical position and never poses anything mid-clip) was blind to the bug and is unchanged by the fix; `dev-verify-clip-relative.mjs` is the gate. The plane + axis are real 3D objects (the body occludes them, which is what makes the plane read as passing THROUGH the joint); the arc is projected and drawn in 2D in the backdrop's ink (a red sagittal sector over red muscle disappears). The auto-frame looks along the axis of motion from the limb's open side, a little off-axis so the plane reads as a plane, at an **exact fit** of the shot's points (the framed joints sampled along the whole motion + arc + plane corners, both halves of a sweep) with a line of headroom for the title and the sides kept clear for the callout columns. **The title block belongs to the dancer, not to the frame**: `titleOrigin` hangs it just above the top of those same shot points, centred on their horizontal span (pinned in the corner it ends up stranded across a wide slide from the anatomy it names), it carries the movement's NAME alone, and the user can drag it anywhere — `studio.titleBox` is where it last drew (canvas px, recorded only for the live overlay, since an export redraws the block at its own size) and `titleHit`/`beginTitleDrag`/`dragTitleTo`/`endTitleDrag` are the seam main.js's pointer handlers drive. `studio.titlePos` keeps the placement as a FRACTION of the frame, so it survives a resize and lands in the same spot at 4K; it is cleared when the stage opens and on exit, and `resetTitlePos()` hands the title back to the dancer. Because the block sits over the picture now, `titleBottom` reserves callout space for it ONLY while it is in the frame's top band — a title dragged down beside the dancer is in the shot, not above it. Timeline: beat at neutral → eased stroke → hold at end range → stroke back → tail, ending on the start pose so it loops seamlessly; "full sweep" appends the `pair` when both share a test position. **The H.264 encoder needs warming up**: the first MP4 `MediaRecorder` sessions of a page record ZERO BYTES with no error (measured ~5.5 s in headless Chrome and Edge, then instant), and so does a codec string whose LEVEL is too low for the frame (1080p60 needs 4.2: `avc1.42E02A` — `avc1.42E01E` passes `isTypeSupported` and writes nothing). `warmUpMp4` runs tiny throwaway sessions until one yields bytes; every recording waits on `whenEncoderReady()` (the ⏺ button reads "Preparing…"), an empty MP4 is never downloaded (it re-records as WebM), and WebM needs none of this.
- `src/draw.js` — floor annotations for teaching diagrams (topbar **Draw** mode): lines, arrows, circles, and text drawn flat on the dance floor as chalk-like meshes (no depth write + polygon offset, and they sit just above the balance hull/trail lines, so nothing z-fights). Each annotation carries its parameters in `userData.annotation`; the rubber-band preview of a half-authored shape lives in a separate `previewGroup` so committed drawings never mix with it. **The annotation IS the drawing**: `#build` is the one place a record becomes geometry, and every edit — a colour, a stroke width, an endpoint — goes `#replace`(edit the record, rebuild), so an authored parameter and what is on screen can never disagree, and a finished shape stays editable instead of having to be redrawn. A record therefore carries its own `color` and `width` (`app.setDrawStyle`, the toolbar's swatch + Size slider; with a drawing SELECTED they restyle that one, otherwise they set the look of the next — one control, two meanings, because a swatch that only ever applies to a shape that does not exist yet is a poor tool). The arrow head and the text height scale with the width, so one slider sets the weight of a whole diagram. **A line and an arrow are SEGMENTS with a movable end each** (a circle its centre + rim, text its position): clicking a committed drawing selects it, `handlePoints`/`moveHandle` give each movable point a handle in `handleGroup`, and dragging one re-shapes the drawing. The handles are **solid balls, not rings or discs** — the ray is cast at whatever the cursor is over and a ring is a hole, so aiming at the middle of one (exactly where a user aims) misses it entirely; and an anchored end floats in space, where a disc lying in the floor plane is seen edge-on and vanishes. **An end can instead be ANCHORED TO A JOINT** (`aAt`/`bAt` = `{ fig, joint }`, resolved in the one place, `endWorld`), which is what makes the floor stop being the only surface a teacher can draw on — a line from the standing hip to the free shoulder has to ride the dancer to go on meaning anything. Such a shape leaves the floor and is built as 3D geometry (a tube, plus a cone for an arrow head) from shared unit meshes, so `updateAnchored` — called from main.js's loop after `syncAtlasNodes`, and only when `anchoredCount` is non-zero — re-places a transform rather than rebuilding geometry. It is drawn THROUGH the dancers (`ANCHORED_RENDER_ORDER`, depth test off): a line about the relationship between two joints that gets buried in the torso on the way past is not doing its job, and the anchors are the depth cue. The joint is read through **`surfacePos`, not `worldPos`** — "where is this dancer physically" is the node the visible body is welded to (the two-frames rule), which also means a click must aim at the PICK SPHERE (a rig node, up to ~6 cm away on a leg) while the anchor lands on the surface node. The floor pair is always filled in beside an anchor, so a dancer that is hidden, or an end dragged back onto the floor, needs no special case. Authoring and the handle drag both take the same two end forms through `drawEnd`/`#end`; Draw mode ghosts the joint pick spheres on hover, because in body view they are invisible and you cannot aim at what you cannot see. Like the clip title's, the grab is armed by HOVER (OrbitControls listens on the same canvas and would already have started a camera rotate by the time our pointerdown ran) and `orbit.enabled` is ASSIGNED every pointermove rather than toggled on the edge. `#replace` puts the rebuilt object back at its own index, because that order is what ⌫ Last removes from. **`#commit` and `refreshHandles` call `updateMatrixWorld` themselves, and must**: raycasting reads `matrixWorld`, which three refreshes at RENDER time, and main.js renders on demand — so a shape or a handle that has just appeared (or been rebuilt mid-edit) is unpickable until a frame happens to go out, and the first hover or click after it silently misses. It presents as intermittent: the check for the endpoint drag failed on one run in four with everything else identical. Handles and the preview are editing chrome and go out of shot with the gizmos (`hideGizmos`); the drawings themselves stay, being the diagram. **A TEXT annotation can leave the floor and float above a dancer**, in the same vocabulary the segments already use: the record gains `posAt: { fig, joint }` beside its `pos` floor pair — a text has ONE position, so it is the a-end case of `#end`/`endWorld`, which now key off `ann[which]`/`ann[\`${which}At\`]` rather than on 'a' vs 'b' — plus a `lift` in metres (`TEXT_LIFT` 0.25, sized so clicking `head` clears the skull). A floor text keeps no `lift` key at all, so every text saved before this is byte-identical. `Drawings.floating()` (anchored **or** lifted) selects the billboard build: an upright quad with `depthTest: false` at `ANCHORED_RENDER_ORDER`, because a caption about a dancer that gets buried in their torso is not doing its job. **The billboard pass is ORIENTATION ONLY** (`updateBillboards`/`#aimBillboard`, fed by `setCamera`) — no rebuild, so the canvas texture is rasterized once — **and it is driven from BOTH branches of main.js's render loop via `refreshBillboards()`, because `anchoredCount` is the wrong gate twice over**: it counts drawings that ride the POSE, while a billboard tracks the CAMERA (an orbit takes the idle branch and skips the solve pass entirely), and a lifted-but-unanchored text rides nothing at all. Verified to fail with the idle-branch call removed (120–149° off facing, against 0.000° with it). The text keeps a **constant WORLD size**: it is GL scene content, so it shrinks with distance like the dancer, the one stroke-width slider goes on setting the weight of the whole diagram, and it needs no re-derivation per export resolution — the problem the overlay solves with fractions of frame height, machinery that does not exist down here. `yaw` is kept for flat floor text and deliberately IGNORED while floating, but is still recorded and still derived from the camera, so dragging the text back down onto the floor lands it readable rather than at an arbitrary angle. `ANCHOR_TOOLS` in main.js (line/arrow/text) replaced the inline type tests, so the Text tool ghosts and aims at joints like the others. The lift is deliberately NOT adjustable from the UI — the handle system is floor-plane oriented and the joint choice is the control that matters; a consequence is that dragging a lifted text's handle moves it in the floor plane and keeps its height. Gate: `dev-verify-draw-text.mjs`. **Floor drawings are SAVED WORK.** `toJSON()`/`fromJSON()` serialize the annotation RECORDS and nothing else, because `#build` is the only path from a record to geometry and an anchored end already stores its dancer as an INDEX for exactly this reason; `fromJSON` validates each record's type and required geometry, so a hand-edited file costs the rows it broke rather than the session, and it bumps the serial past every incoming id BEFORE minting any. Every record carries a stable **`id`**, minted once in `#styled` and carried through by the `...ann` spread that `restyle` and `moveHandle` already build their new records with — `#replace` makes a new Object3D from the SAME record, so a restyle or an endpoint drag cannot orphan a keyframe's reference to that drawing. `ui.js` persists the whole diagram under `tangoPoseStudio.drawings.v1` from `onDrawingsChanged`, the one hook every mutation already passes through, behind a `drawingsReady` guard the Muscles panel's look also uses — **the floor is EMPTY at startup and the restore writes through the very hook that saves, so without the guard the first sync overwrites the saved set with nothing**. `setVisibleIds(ids|null)` filters the committed children only (never `previewGroup`/`handleGroup`), `#replace` re-stamps the filter on the rebuilt object, and `pickAt` filters to what is SHOWN — three's raycaster does not consult `visible`, so a hidden drawing would otherwise still take a click. The filter is view state and is deliberately NOT persisted: only the records are, so a reload shows the whole diagram until a keyframe says otherwise. Gate: `dev-verify-draw-persist.mjs`. Static look tuning (chalk default, arrow head proportions, text height) is the constants at the top.
- `src/main.js` — scene, picking, gizmos, interaction modes (rotate joint / drag hand-foot / move / turn / pivot-on-foot / pin spots / draw / label), undo stack, balance visuals, wooden floor; **keyboard nudges** drive whatever is active (one small change per keydown, so the OS key-repeat makes holding continuous; Shift = coarser): Move/Step figures as before, a selected joint's free axes (↑↓ = X, ←→ = Z else Y, PageUp/Down = Y twist, through `app.editJoint` so closed chain holds), or the live drag handle — IK target / swivel pole / caress ring / hips — moved camera-relative in the floor plane + PageUp/Down vertical, re-solved by `applyHandleChange` (shared with the gizmo's objectChange); **Move-hips auto-selects**: entering the mode seats the hips handle on the last-touched (else first visible) dancer without a click — clicking the other dancer moves it, empty-space clicks keep it, and it re-seats when its dancer is hidden or a preset is applied; A→B pose interpolation (`lerpPose`, `applyInterp`, `playInterp`) with a COG floor trail — every interpolation path (`applyStatesT`: A→B, sequence, trail, video export) **grounds planted feet**: a foot on the floor at both ends of a segment stays connected to it throughout (holding its spot, or gliding along the floor when the keyframes place it differently), the leg's hip/knee/ankle re-solving from the lerped pose (`stateFeet`/`groundInterpFeet`; `app.interpGroundFeet = false` restores the raw lerp) — translucent ghost figures of snapshots (`setGhost`), and `pivotFigure` (rotate about the ball of the support foot; combines with "Move as couple" for a calesita). **Move mode slides AND turns from ONE gizmo** — no Slide/Turn toggle. `selectFigure` attaches TWO `TransformControls` to the figure's group: `tcontrols` in translate mode (the floor-plane slide arrows, as before) and a second `turnControls` in rotate-Y mode (a yaw ring, `size 1.0` so it encircles the arrows). Grab the arrows to slide, the ring to turn — the same handle does both, which is what the split toggle used to gate. The ring is a separate instance because three.js `TransformControls` shows one mode at a time; it has its OWN `dragging-changed` (which pushes undo history and calls `beginFigureTurn`) and `objectChange` (`applyFigureTurn`), since the main `tcontrols` handlers never fire for it. Which vertical axis a turn spins about is `app.movePivot` (`setMovePivot`, the "Turn about" dropdown — the one control that survived the toggle removal), resolved by `turnPivot()` and used by the ring and the arrow keys alike: `'foot'` the ball of the support foot (the ground-referenced tango pivot — ocho, giro — the default), `'cog'` the axis through the centre of gravity (balance-referenced; the only axis a turn on both feet can use, and with "Move as couple" it is the pair's SHARED COG from `coupleReport`, the axis a giro actually orbits), `'root'` the figure's own origin. The gizmo spins the group about its own origin, so the `figTurn` capture (drag start, in `beginFigureTurn`) records the pivot plus both dancers' start transforms and `applyFigureTurn` re-derives every frame absolutely from them — never incrementally, so a long drag cannot drift. The **movement sequence** (`app.seqStates` + `seqAdd`/`seqUpdate`/`seqMove`/`seqDelete`/`applySeqT`/`playSeq`; sidebar "Sequence" section) generalizes A→B to any keyframe chain: `applyStatesT(states, t)` lerps segment-wise, the COG trail traces whichever chain `trailStates()` picks (the sequence when it has ≥2 keyframes, else A/B), and the current sequence persists in localStorage with JSON export/import. **Each keyframe carries TWO numbers** — `move`, the seconds of transition from the previous keyframe INTO this one, and `hold`, the seconds the couple then STAYS in that pose (`seqSetTravel`/`seqTravel`, `seqSetHold`/`seqHold`, `seqSeconds`; the API says `travel` only because `seqMove(i, di)` is already the REORDER). A movement is not only travel: a teacher wants the couple to ARRIVE and then be looked at. The timeline is hold₀, move₁, hold₁, move₂, hold₂, …; the FIRST keyframe has no move (nothing precedes it — its box is disabled with the reason in its tooltip) and the LAST one does have a hold, which is how a recording lingers on the end pose instead of cutting the instant it arrives. `t` is a fraction of the chain's RUNNING TIME including holds, not of its segment count, so a keyframe held longer occupies proportionally more of the scrubber and of a recording. **With every hold 0 and every move equal this reduces EXACTLY to the old equal-time split, and that reduction is the regression guard** (`dev-verify-sequence.mjs` checks t = 0.5 of a three-keyframe chain still lands on the middle keyframe) — which is why `SEQ_HOLD_SECONDS` is 0. The exactness is not a tolerance, it is the walk in `seqTimeMap`: it advances on a **strict `>`** at every phase boundary, so an exact boundary still resolves as u = 1 of the segment just finished — the arithmetic that used to run. **The legacy field is `dur`** ("seconds to reach the NEXT keyframe", i.e. keyframe i's `dur` is keyframe i+1's `move`), migrated ONCE in `setSeqStates` (`normalizeSeqTiming`) — the single entry point the localStorage restore and the JSON import both go through — because `dur` belongs to the gap AFTER a row while `move` belongs to the keyframe, so only the migrated form survives a REORDER with the right owner. It is carried across UNCLAMPED (a migration that quietly changes a number cannot be trusted; measured, a 6/2/3 legacy chain reproduces the old arithmetic at six values of t with a worst error of 0.000°), and `dur` is deleted only after every keyframe has read its predecessor's. A read-time fallback stays in `seqTiming` for a chain that never passed through there — every A/B pair, every chain a script builds by hand — which keeps `applyStatesT([A, B], t)` byte-identical; `seqSetDuration`/`seqDuration` survive as shims (`duration(i)` ≡ `travel(i+1)`). `seqUpdate` re-records the POSE only, spreading the fresh couple state over the old keyframe (`{ ...old, ...getCoupleState(old.name) }`) so `move`, `hold`, `name` and **any field another feature hung there** survive by construction rather than by being enumerated. **`applyStatesT` is split in two**: `seqTimeMap` (time → segment + fraction + which keyframe's extras) and `applyStatesU(states, i, u)` (the pose at that parameter, with no reference to the clock). The split exists for the COG trail: a trail is a PATH, and sampled by TIME it would spend its ink wherever the couple is standing still, so `updateCogTrail` calls `applyStatesU` directly at 32 samples per SEGMENT — a hold costs it nothing, and with no holds it is byte-identical to the old sampling. **A row is `index · label · Show · ⟳ · ⧉ · ✎ · ✕`, with a `.seq-time` line (`into … s   hold … s`) and a `.seq-extras` line for the caption, the `◻ Drawings` (`.seq-draw-btn`) and `◻ Muscles` (`.seq-kf-btn`) tags and the name/caption eye + swatch pairs (`.seq-ink`)** (the two timing boxes left the controls row because two number boxes in a 320 px row squeeze each below the width "2.4" needs): the leading index stays (it is what maps a row onto the movement scrubber) and is also the drag grip, the label is the keyframe's own `state.name` edited in place (`seqSetName`; blank falls back to a `Keyframe N` placeholder so no row is anonymous). Rows reorder by DRAGGING, committed through `seqMoveTo(from, to)` — a **lift-and-drop, not a run of swaps**: swapping row 1 up to position 5 would carry each row it passed one step backwards with it, rewriting five rows instead of one, so `seqMoveTo` splices the row out and back in and `seqMove(i, ±1)` now delegates to it. Because a pointer gesture is unreachable by keyboard, each row is focusable and Alt+↑/↓ performs the same move with focus following the row. Two traps are encoded in the row: a label field torn out by a re-render fires its `change` **synchronously, before it is detached**, so `isConnected` is useless and a render-generation counter is what stops it writing stale text back over a fresh rename; and a pointer drag over rows is a text SELECTION to the browser, which is why `.seq-item` is `user-select: none` with the label opting back in. **A keyframe may also carry an optional `kf` block — `caption`, `muscles` and `draw`** — merged through the single `setKfField` helper (the block is never rebuilt, an emptied one is deleted, an unknown key rides through untouched). `applyKeyframeExtras` applies each from the keyframe being travelled **FROM**, holding until the next is reached, because a diagram or caption that re-picked itself per frame would flicker through a recorded video; `applyStatesT(states, t, { extras = true })` gives the scrubber, both players and `recordPlayback` the same answer, while `updateCogTrail` passes `extras: false` because its ~289 replays per edit are sampling a path, not showing a keyframe. `seqApply(i)` applies extras too, being the one timeline seam that bypasses `applyStatesT`. Absent means "inherit whatever the user has running", so every keyframe authored before this behaves identically. Four more flat keys ride in `kf`: **`nameColor` / `nameHidden` / `captionColor` / `captionHidden`** (`app.seqSetTextStyle`/`seqTextStyle`), each written through `setKfField` on its own so clearing a colour cannot take the hide with it and an untouched keyframe serializes byte-identically. **Hiding is applied by handing the studio an EMPTY STRING**, not a flag — one rule that also keeps the block out of every export and makes it un-grabbable. **`seqAdd` stores NO name** (`getCoupleState('')`): the row's placeholder and `seqName(i)` already fall back to the index, but a stored `Keyframe 3` is that placeholder frozen into the file — stale after the first reorder and stamped over a recorded video as if the user wrote it. `screenName`/`AUTO_NAME` treat `/^Keyframe \d+$/` as no name for ON-SCREEN purposes only. **Placement is ONE setting for the whole sequence and colour/hide are PER KEYFRAME** (the user's call, and not an accident: a caption that jumped corners at every keyframe would make a recorded lesson unwatchable, while the ink is part of what a keyframe is saying); without that the row marker reports no current keyframe and a focus session ends silently on the most natural gesture in it (focus, adjust the pose, re-record). `⧉` (`seqDuplicate(i)`) drops a **deep JSON copy** in at `i+1` — nothing may be shared, or editing the copy's caption or drawing tag rewrites the original's — keeping the name, and moves "current" onto the copy only when it duplicated the current row. **"+ Add keyframe" inserts AFTER THE CURRENT keyframe** (`seqInsertIndex`, resolved in ONE place as `seqAdd`'s default index), appending only when there is none; "Current" is `seqShownIndex()` — the keyframe whose extras are showing — so the scrubber, the players, the marker and Add share one notion of it. A mid-chain insert gets the plain default `move` and deliberately does NOT split its neighbour's travel time. The remap lives on the TIME → pose-parameter path (`easeU`, called from `applyStatesT`) and **never inside `applyStatesU`**: the COG trail samples `applyStatesU`, so easing changes WHEN the couple is where and never WHERE it goes — the trail is byte-identical on and off, and `at` is still decided by the time phase. **The curve is smoothstep, taken whole or by halves, under the REST RULE** (`restsOn`): a segment eases out of keyframe *i* only if the couple was at rest there (the first keyframe, or `hold` > 0) and into *i+1* only if it will rest there (the last, or a hold), so a pass-through keyframe is crossed at speed and a giro flows instead of stuttering stop-go-stop-go. A one-sided segment takes the half of that same S it needs (`2·S(u/2)`, `2·S(0.5+u/2)−1`), so both free ends hand over at the same 1.5×; every branch is exactly 0 at u=0 and 1 at u=1 in floating point, so arrivals and holds stay byte-exact. A velocity step remains at a pass-through whose two segments differ in duration or distance — accepted, it is what linear does, and removing it means a spline THROUGH the keyframes, which would move the path. **smootherstep was measured and rejected** (peak per-frame travel 1.875× the mean vs 1.500×, and 0.23 s vs 0.13 s parked within a degree of the keyframe — it reads as hesitation). **The flag is RESOLVED BY THE CALLER** (`applyStatesT(…, { ease })`, `recordPlayback(states, name, { ease })`), never sniffed from the array, so the A→B compare stays linear. **Defaults are compatibility**: OFF for a session restored without the key and for a file/bundle with no `ease`; ON for a timeline the user starts EMPTY (`SEQ_EASE_FRESH`) unless someone has already said otherwise (`seqEaseChosen`). `dev-verify-sequence.mjs`'s reduction guards set it OFF explicitly, being checks of the linear arithmetic. **A keyframe can be edited ON ITS OWN (the ✎ edit focus), and drawing ownership is a UNION.** `app.seqFocus(i|null)` / `seqFocusIndex()`: the couple stands at that keyframe, every drawing committed from then on is filed under it (`kf.own`, appended by `app.claimForFocus` from `afterDrawAdded` — the ONE place a new drawing lands), and every Muscles-panel edit writes its `kf.muscles` instead of the running look (ui.js's `lookTarget()` seam). A duplicated keyframe shares its original's ids and both show the drawing, which is why `seqDelete` drops only ORPHANS and why its Undo holds the records in its closure (`Drawings.restore`). The `◻ Drawings` capture stores the shown ids MINUS the owned ones. Focus is tracked by state IDENTITY (a reorder keeps it), is **session state** — never persisted or serialized — and is left by ✎ again, Esc (below the half-finished things, above the selection), focusing or Showing another row, the scrubber, a player, a recording, Present, Add / ⧉-of-the-focused-row (the playhead moves to a new keyframe), or loading/clearing/importing the chain. The durable indicator is the row's pressed ✎ plus a banner in the Sequence section and one-liners in the Muscles panel and the Draw toolbar — `app.status` clears after ~3 s and a focus session lasts as long as the user spends drawing. `kf.muscles` stores `lit` + `colors` only, so the Muscles panel's hidden set and tint stay global while focused. The section lives on the **Teach** tab — a keyframe timeline is what a lesson video is built from, not a measurement. **Animation export**: `app.recordPlayback(states, name)` replays a keyframe chain while capturing via `MediaRecorder`, downloading a `.webm` — the ⏺ buttons in the Compare (A→B) and Sequence sections. It goes through `studio.startRecorder`, whose per-frame `studio.renderFrame()` **composites the GL and hud canvases** into the canvas `MediaRecorder` captures, so labels and captions really are in a recorded sequence video (pinned in PIXELS by `dev-verify-keyframe-extras.mjs`, not assumed — capturing the GL canvas alone would drop every overlay silently). The **dissociation floor visual** (`DissociationViz`; View → "Dissociation" checkbox) draws each dancer's floor-projected hip axis (their color) and shoulder axis (white) under the pelvis with a translucent wedge sweeping the signed twist between them — the picture of the `tangoStats` dissociation number, clearest in the Top view. The **walk** (`app.stepFigure` → `beginStep`/`poseStep`/`finalizeStep`) is a gait-phase step: it ends on the classic contact pose (front foot landed heel-first, trailing foot peeled onto the ball, pelvis yawed ~6° into the step with the chest counter-yawed — tango dissociation; the yaw SUM is preserved so authored trunk twists survive) and animates there through a floor-caressing collection (`app.stepAnims`, advanced in the render loop; `app.animateSteps=false` snaps). `plantFoot` reaches a leg's ankle to a flat-sole target and pitches the foot **about its ground contact** (ball for a roll, heel for a heel-strike — the ankle target swings along that arc; the roll is capped so the leg stays out near its reach instead of folding the knee), then nudges the target until the foot's lowest sole corner (`Figure.footLowY`) sits exactly on the floor. The body's along-travel position is **anchored to the support foot each step** (ending `STEP_ADVANCE` of the stride past it, preserving the dancer's own stance offset so a leaning couple isn't snapped apart) — accumulating a fixed advance instead drifts the body behind the feet until the front landing is out of reach. A linked partner steps the initiator's stride AND the mirrored foot (his left pairs her right; same-letter feet sit on opposite rails and jam leg-on-leg). Mid-stride the close-embrace couple legitimately rests a few cm wider than standing contact where the interleaved thighs brush (`COLLIDERS`). The **toe caress** (`caressToe`; clicking a toes joint in Drag-limb mode starts it, `app.caressFoot` scripts it) drags a leg by a floor ring while the big toe's pad stays exactly ON the floor: flat sole under the body rolling up to a pointed toe as the leg reaches (lápiz/tendu), hip/knee/ankle/toes absorbing everything. The pinned contact is the toe-PAD center (all other sole corners share its plane or sit behind it), so the sole never penetrates and the caress can never lift the body; when joint limits stop the toe reaching the floor at the cursor, a bisection along the ray from below the hip keeps it grounded at the farthest reachable point (reachability is monotone on that ray — NOT along the drag path, which crosses a tucked-under-the-body zone the solver can't ground). The **Move hips** mode (`app.moveHips`; the topbar "Planted" checkboxes → `app.hipsPlant`, auto-set from floor contact on pick) drags a pelvis handle on all three axes: the upper body rides rigidly (no posture change), feet marked planted keep their world position AND sole orientation via leg IK (the closed-chain hip-height slider now routes through the same code), unplanted legs ride along. **A rise never pulls a planted foot off the floor** (`groundPlantedLeg`): as the hips go up the planted leg first straightens (knee/hip extend), then the foot rolls up onto its toe (the ankle plantarflexes — a relevé) so the front sole still rests on the floor, and once even a full relevé can't hold the foot down the pelvis stops rising there — a Newton back-off inside `moveHips` clamps the height to where the most-lifted planted foot still reaches (past the limit it rises ~1:1 with the pelvis). The relevé measures from a base frozen at grab time (`captureHipsPlantBase` → `hipsState.plantBase`, captured in the hips-handle `dragging-changed`), so a rise-then-lower drag reverses cleanly (the foot flattens back down) instead of ratcheting; one-shot callers (the hip-height slider, keyboard nudges, scripts) fall back to a live snapshot per call. Verified by `dev-verify-hips-rise.mjs`. Move hips also offers **Slide or Twist** (its own sub-toolbar → `app.setHipsTool`); Twist swaps the handle for a yaw ring driving `app.pivotHips`, **the ocho-pivot dissociation**: the pelvis yaws and the whole lower body goes with it — hips, legs and feet turn as one unit, which is free since they hang off the pelvis node — while the spine twists to pay for it, the chest and lumbar joints counter-yawing by the same total so the shoulders, head and embrace keep facing exactly where they were (the yaw-sum-preserving idiom the walk's dissociation uses; exact upright, drifting slightly under a large pelvis tilt where the two yaws sit in non-parallel frames). The pelvis yaw is **clamped to the trunk's remaining counter-twist** (chest ±35° spent first — thoracic rotation is where tango dissociation lives — then the lumbar spine's ±8°, so ~43° from neutral, about a real dancer's range): past that the trunk has nothing left, and turning further would saturate the chest and start carrying the shoulders round with the hips, the one thing the move is defined by not doing. The "Planted" checkboxes belong to the SLIDE and are hidden in Twist — this move turns the feet on purpose. **Hover feedback** (rotate / drag-limb modes): the joint pick spheres are invisible at rest in body view AND sit inside the opaque avatar, so a glow alone never reaches the screen. Hovering a dancer there ghosts that dancer's whole joint set through the skin (`GHOST_OPACITY`) and lights the one under the cursor amber — `material.depthTest = false` is what draws them through the body, `renderOrder` keeps them above it, and `Figure.setLayers` restores both so a layer switch can't strand spheres floating in front. Skeleton view keeps its flat 0.22 look and normal depth sorting (the spheres already sit over visible bones). `app.photoDataURL`/`app.capturePhoto` snapshot the canvas to PNG with every gizmo/handle hidden. **Draw mode** authors floor annotations two clicks at a time on the y=0 plane (Text: one click + prompt, oriented to read right-way-up from the current camera; Esc, a mode change, or a click off the floor abandons a half-drawn shape); scriptable via `app.addDrawLine/addDrawArrow/addDrawCircle/addDrawText` + `removeLastDrawing`/`clearDrawings` (`app.drawings` lists them, `app.draw` is the Drawings instance). Annotations are scene content, deliberately OUTSIDE the pose undo stack — the Draw toolbar's ⌫ Last / Clear manage them. **The COG drop line is a TUBE, not a `THREE.Line`**: WebGL ignores `LineBasicMaterial.linewidth` on every desktop driver, so a line has exactly one width and "draw it thicker" is not expressible at all. It is a unit cylinder scaled per frame, with the dashes coming from an alpha map whose repeat is re-derived from the line's length so a dash stays the same size however tall the COG sits. That also makes it something a ray can HIT: clicking the line selects it (`app.selectCogLine`, `cogLineSelected`) and the View panel's width slider + colour swatch then act on that one dancer instead of all three — the panel names its target, and Esc deselects. `setCogLineStyle` applies the radius immediately rather than waiting for the next balance pass, since the viz only updates on a solve frame and a slider that does nothing until the dancer next moves reads as broken. A line's colour defaults to the dancer's identity colour (what the ball, the hull and the stats all use to say WHOSE balance this is), so Reset is an explicit opt-out rather than a value to be overwritten. **COG click-to-front**: clicking a COG BALL toggles drawing that whole indicator (ball, drop line, floor marker) through the dancers (`BalanceViz.setFront`: depth test off + late render order — the ball otherwise hides inside/behind the bodies, where a click couldn't even find it); an in-front ball wins the click outright (it is what the user sees), otherwise the nearest of ball vs. joint/figure hit wins, so ordinary picking is unaffected. `app.setCogHighlight(on, which?)` / `app.cogHighlight()` script it.
- `src/ui.js` — side panel: the **View** section's layer picker is a single `#layer-mode` dropdown (`LAYER_MODES`), three mutually exclusive views rather than three checkboxes: `body` (the opaque clothed avatar alone), `skeleton` (bones alone), `muscle` (muscles over the skeleton — the bellies need the bones behind them to read as anatomy). Body never combines with the other two because it is opaque and hides them. Scripts drive it by setting `#layer-mode`.value + dispatching `change`; `Figure.setLayers` still takes the three booleans, so `dev-verify-alignment.mjs` can still ask for the skeleton+body overlay directly. Also: sliders, stats, A/B compare with movement scrubber + ghost toggles + ⏺ Record, the Sequence section (keyframe list with Show/⟳/↑/↓/✕ rows, whole-movement scrubber, Play, ⏺ Record video, JSON export/import, localStorage persistence), the Contact pins section (pin list + Clear all; authoring happens in the 3D view), pose library (localStorage + JSON export/import); the **Muscles** panel gives each belly a highlight chip AND its own colour swatch (shown only while it is lit, like the Highlight panel's part swatches), plus one "Colour strength" slider over the lot (`app.setMuscleTint`, 100% = exactly the colour picked) — `applyMuscleColor` is the ONE place a muscle's colour changes, whichever control asked, and it takes a group because a clip's callout names several bellies; the same function serves `pickMuscleColor`, the hook main.js calls when a lit belly is clicked in the 3D view, which opens a floating `<input type="color">` under the cursor (a `display:none` input has no picker to show, so it is a rendered element at zero size — `.floating-picker`). **The panel's whole look persists** (`tangoPoseStudio.muscleLook.v1` — colours, lit set, hidden set, strength; `saveMuscleLook`/`restoreMuscleLook`, with `muscleLookReady` holding writes off until the stored look has been applied, the same guard the sidebar layout uses): a colour picked for a belly is authored work — it is what ties a lit muscle to the callout naming it on a slide — and a refresh mid-lesson threw the set away. Colours are restored whether or not their belly is lit, so lighting it again finds the colour it was given rather than the default amber; `applyViewState` re-saves, so showing a slide makes that slide's look the running one. Slides still capture the look too, but this is the running state, so the colours are there on the next load without having to save a slide first. **A sequence keyframe's `kf.muscles` is a VIEW OVERRIDE that never reaches storage** (`muscleOverride` / `pushMuscleLook` / `effLit`/`effColors`): `applyViewState` deliberately ends with `saveMuscleLook()` because showing a SLIDE makes that slide's look the running one, but a scrub is not a slide — the playhead crosses every keyframe on the way past, and persisting each one would grind the panel state the user authored down to whichever keyframe it last sat on. Colours are cleared by DIFFERENCE (tracked in `appliedColors`) so dropping an override restores exactly the running ones, a `sameLook` early-out keeps it cheap while a sequence plays, the chips render from the EFFECTIVE sets so the panel cannot lie about the scene, and any panel edit, a slide, or Clear hands the running look back. Because lit bellies now render in skeleton view too, a per-keyframe highlight works there as well. **The Sequence section holds a sequence LIBRARY** (the "Saved sequences" `.sub-block` at its head; `tangoPoseStudio.seqLibrary.v1` name→bundle, `tangoPoseStudio.seqLibOrder.v1` — its own key, so a sequence may be called "order" — and `tangoPoseStudio.seqCurrent.v1` for which entry the timeline corresponds to). **A saved sequence is a BUNDLE, and there is exactly ONE capture and ONE apply** (`captureSeqBundle` / `applySeqBundle`): `{ states, drawings, textPos, ease }` — the keyframes, the floor diagram their `kf.draw`/`kf.own` ids point at, the one placement of each on-screen text, and the ease setting. **The file Export/Import routes through the same pair**, so a file and a library entry are the same object inside an `{app:'tangle', type:'sequence', version:1}` envelope; keys stay additive, and a legacy file (a bare array, or no `drawings`/`textPos`/`ease`) imports exactly as before — `drawings: null` leaves the floor alone, absent `ease` is OFF. `applySeqBundle` owns the ordering: `pushHistory`, the drawings BEFORE the keyframes, `setSeqStates` (where the legacy `dur` migration lives), `setSeqEase` AFTER the chain and explicitly even when false (`setSeqStates` leaves the previous sequence's choice standing for a non-empty chain), then `clearKeyframeExtras` — the caption/muscle override/draw filter on screen belong to the sequence being REPLACED. **Deep-copied both ways, and that is not belt-and-braces**: `setSeqStates` keeps the array it is handed and `normalizeSeqTiming` mutates it in place. **The dirty marker (•) is a CONTENT comparison, not an edited-since flag** (undoing an edit by hand clears it), computed part by part against cached strings: `renderSequence` already builds the states string for `SEQ_KEY`, and the check runs per pointermove of a drawing-handle drag — hence `syncSeqLibCurrent` (heading + quick-Save, no storage read) vs `renderSeqLibrary` (the list). The scripted API (`app.seqBundle`/`setSeqBundle`/`seqLibNames`/`seqLibEntry`/`seqLibSave`/`seqLibLoad`/`seqLibDelete`/`seqLibCurrent`/`seqLibNew`) **never opens a dialog**; Load and New ask only when dirty, `Save as` only when overwriting, the quick `Save "…"` and a single Delete never (Delete offers a status-line Undo restoring the entry AND its place). **`saveSeqLib` REPORTS a full quota** (`app.status(…, 'error')`, naming Export file) and writes the order only once the bundle is stored; the running timeline's own `SEQ_KEY` write still swallows a full quota knowingly (it fires per keystroke, and the work is still on screen). A collapsible block INSIDE a section is a `.sub-block` + `.sub-toggle`, never a nested `<section>` — the tab filter, the `last-shown` hairline and the layout store all walk `#sidebar > section`. Scripted-caller trap: `app.drawings` is the RECORD list; `app.draw.group.children` are the Object3Ds, and `selectDrawing` handed a record selects NOTHING. The joint picker is a two-column grid (center joints as a chip row, then Left/Right PAIRED per row so the side lives in the column header and the list is half as long); the **Foot map** section draws a top-down canvas of one foot as a classic ink foot print — an hourglass sole (deep medial arch waist, rounded heel) whose forefoot is **beveled and oblique** — the metatarsal break runs diagonally (the 1st MTP head sits forward of the 5th) and each ball corner chamfers into the toe crease via an anchor riding the straight MTP→crease chord, which keeps the corner flat instead of rounding off under the Catmull-Rom smoothing. The crease sits only just ahead of the metatarsal heads, because the toes own nearly the whole toe region (a hallux is ~14% of foot length against a ~16% toe region) and the corner cage's toe region is barely half the ball width — a crease pushed further forward eats the space the pads need. Five tilted toe ovals sit ahead of it, spaced anatomically: each at a fixed cross-station of the ball width (a real toe row spans the full forefoot breadth, hallux centered ~16% in from the medial edge … little toe ~92%), tips on the retreating toe arc (hallux at the tip line down to ~56% of the toe region for the little toe, from anthropometric toe lengths), pad centers hung back from their tips, and long axes radiating toward the heel (the big toe near-parallel to the foot axis, lateral toes tilting toward the midline). The row clears the sole by **construction**, not tuned constants: the sole's front edge is modelled across the foot by its three front anchors, and the whole row shifts forward by ONE shared amount, so a stubbier shoe (the follower's `soleScale`) or a foreshortened pointed foot can't sink pads into the sole and the arc keeps its shape. It is all hung on that figure's fitted corner cage so it stretches/turns/foreshortens with the real foot — with dashed heel|arch and arch|ball separators clipped to the print, heel/arch/ball/toes section labels along the print's own axis (the arch label offset laterally by half the waist dent so it doesn't hang over the medial edge; both labels and dashes hidden when the print draws too small to hold them), and the COG drop point over it, green inside/red outside, heel/mid-foot/ball zone in the note, refreshed on the stats tick (the balance hull itself still uses the raw corners, so the verdict stays honest while the print is stylized). **The print is drawn in the SOLE'S OWN PLANE** — the foot viewed perpendicular to its sole — not projected flat onto the floor. Flattening foreshortens a pitched foot into a blob (measured: a pointed foot's 27.6 cm corner cage collapsed to 4.4 cm), which is what made the print stop resembling the foot on screen and threw off where the COG read against it. The frame is a Newell best-fit normal over the corner cage (the corners are only near-coplanar once the toes bend), `fwd` = heel→toe flattened into that plane, `latAxis = fwd × normal`. **When the sole is flat the normal IS +Y and this reduces exactly to the old floor projection**, so a standing figure draws byte-identically — that reduction property is the regression guard, not a tolerance. The COG is the 3D COG dropped along the same normal (again identical to the old floor drop when flat). The **balance verdict deliberately stays a FLOOR question**: `margin`/hull still come from the corners' floor projection, the same base of support the 3D view outlines, so the green/red reading is unaffected by the drawing change. When only part of the sole is grounded (heel up, pointed foot) the contacting corners are filled as a dashed green **contact patch** — the same region the 3D support outline shows — and the note says so. Orientation: toes up (v = heel→toe), and the lateral axis is **anatomically fixed per foot** — the print is a footprint on the floor seen from above with the foot pointing away, so a RIGHT foot always draws with its big toe/arch on the canvas LEFT (medial) and a LEFT foot on the right, each reading as the correct foot regardless of camera or which way the dancer faces. This falls out of the single `toUV` projection because the two feet's medial ("in") corners lie on opposite world sides of the foot axis; no per-side sign is needed. **Do NOT tie u to the camera** to make the dot match the on-screen left/right: mirroring the frame to match the view turns a right foot's print into a left foot's (tried and reverted). The COG dot shares this projection, so it always lands on that foot's true medial/lateral side — read the dot against the drawn big toe/arch, not against the 3D screen. **Verified**: a sweep of all 12 presets × both dancers × both feet found the dot on the correct anatomical side in 48/48 cases, and `u` is provably screen-right for a camera looking straight down (`right = up × back = (fx,0,fz) × (0,1,0) = (-fz,0,fx)`), so the frame IS a true bird's-eye view. Beware when comparing against the 3D viewport: seen from the FRONT a dancer's medial side appears on the viewer's right, but the map (a print on the floor seen from above) puts it on the canvas left — the two legitimately mirror, and that is not a bug. Note also that the drawn print is stylized and INSET from the corner cage (the arch waist pulls the medial border in by 0.26·ballW), while the dot and the green/red verdict are measured against the raw cage — so the dot can sit visibly outside the inked arch while still being inside the balance hull. Also owns the topbar Draw sub-toolbar (line/arrow/circle/text pick + ⌫ Last / Clear, shown only in Draw mode).

### The sidebar's workflow tabs, slides, and Present mode

- **The sidebar is three tabs, not one column** (`Pose` / `Teach` / `Measure`). Sections do NOT move to change tab: each `<section>` declares its group with `data-tab` and CSS filters on `#sidebar`'s `data-active-tab`, so DOM order stays visual order and focus order with it. `activateTab` (ui.js) also marks `.last-shown`, because the dividing hairline belongs to the last *shown* section and `:last-child` cannot express that under a `display:none` filter. The strip is plain toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`, deliberately not `role="tab"` — without one wrapping element per group there is no honest tabpanel for `aria-controls`. Active tab + the folded set persist under `tangoPoseStudio.layout.v1`. **`setSectionCollapsed(section, false)` activates that section's tab**, so any code that reveals a section (the joint panel on selection, Contact pins on a pending spot, Labels after "Label highlighted") cannot unfold it inside a hidden group.
- **A saved entry is a SLIDE: the pose plus how it is shown.** `getCoupleState(name, { view: true })` adds a `view` block — layer, backdrop, frame, which dancers are shown, the camera + orbit target, `labels.toJSON()`, the highlighted parts with their colours, and the muscle hidden/lit sets (captured by `ui.getViewState`, restored by `ui.applyViewState`). The deck's running order lives under its own key (`tangoPoseStudio.slideOrder.v1`) rather than inside the name→slide map, which would collide with a slide actually called "order"; names missing from it fall in alphabetically at the end, so nothing a user saved can become unreachable.
- **Present mode** (`app.enterPresent`/`exitPresent`/`togglePresent`, topbar ▶ Present) hides the sidebar, topbar and hint via `#app.presenting`, forces the 16:9 frame, and requests fullscreen. It also takes the **joint pick spheres** off the slide (`app.setPickSpheresVisible` → `Figure.setPickVisible`, restored on exit): in the skeleton and muscle views they are translucent blobs ringing every joint, which is the difference between a slide of a dancer and a slide of the tool. The flag lives on the Figure and `setLayers` honours it, so a layer switch mid-deck cannot write the resting 0.22 back; `styleSphere` in main.js checks it too, so hover, selection and the limit amber cannot re-light one either. They stay clickable throughout — raycasting ignores opacity. The status line stays — with the sidebar gone it is how a slide change announces itself — and re-centres at the foot of the screen. `app.gotoSlide(±1)` steps the deck; its ends are **walls, not a wrap**. A `fullscreenchange` listener exits Present when fullscreen is left by a route the page never sees (the browser eats Esc, and F11 never reaches JS), or the user would be stranded with no visible chrome. **A video capture enters Present mode for its duration** (`app.recordPlayback`), so a recorded sequence carries no pick spheres and is framed at exactly 1920×1080; while a capture runs, Esc means "stop the recording and save it", not "leave the slide", and the deck keys are inert.

## Behavioral rules (deliberate design decisions — do not "fix")

- **A constraint that refuses must SAY SO — `app.status(text, kind)` and the amber.** For a tool whose selling point is anatomical limits, a limit the 3D view never mentions reads as a broken app rather than an anatomical one. There is one non-modal notice region (`#status-line` in index.html, styled beside `#hint`; `setStatus` in main.js, kinds `'info' | 'limit' | 'error'`, auto-clearing after ~3 s and coalescing identical repeats so it is free to call from inside a drag — it is in `RENDER_WRAP_SKIP`, so it never wakes the solve loop). **Never `alert()`**: it blocks a class to say something the user can only act on afterwards. Being DOM chrome, the status line is structurally incapable of reaching an export — `studio.photoDataURL` / `startRecorder` composite the GL and overlay canvases and never read the page. In the 3D view the vocabulary is ONE amber, `STRAIN_COLOR` (exported from `pins.js`, mirrored as `--strain` in style.css): a strained contact pin, a joint at its limit (the pick sphere's emissive — read inside `styleSphere` so hover/selection/layer restyles preserve it), and an out-of-reach drag handle plus its strain line (`makeStrainLine`/`setStrainLine`, factored out of `ContactPins.updateVisuals` and reused by `applyHandleChange`). `HOVER_EMISSIVE` stays a separate, slightly different amber — it means "under your cursor", not "anatomy says no".
- **A destructive action confirms only when there is something to lose — and the dialog lives in the UI layer, never in `app.*`.** The undo stack holds COUPLE POSES only (`pushHistory` → `getCoupleState`), so it can recover none of this: a wiped keyframe timeline, a released pin set, cleared labels, a deleted library pose. Two shapes, chosen by what is at stake. A BULK wipe (`#seq-clear`, `#pin-clear`, `#label-clear`, an import over an existing sequence) asks with `window.confirm` — the one case the status line cannot serve, because it is a question — and only when the count is non-zero, so the empty case stays one click. A SINGLE-item delete (`seqDelete`, the pose library's ✕) does NOT interrupt: it deletes and offers a clickable Undo on the status line for 6 s (`app.status(text, kind, { label, run })`), because a dialog per row is worse than the loss it prevents. **The confirm must sit in the `ui.js` click handler, not in the `app` method** — `dev-verify-pins`/`-studio`/`-sequence` call `app.clearPins()` / `clearLabels()` / `setSeqStates([])` directly, and a dialog inside those would hang every headless run (the scripts read `#pin-clear.disabled`, they never click it). Also: the height field in the Dancers panel takes a `pushHistory` BEFORE resizing (`applyCoupleState` restores `meta.heights`, so undo then puts the height back too — without the snapshot Ctrl+Z replayed the old pose onto the new height) and a `markEdit`, like every other path that changes a dancer.
- **Camera presets follow the DANCERS, and there is exactly one camera fitter.** `setView` keeps its four fixed offsets but measures them from `sceneCenter()` (the mean floor position of the SHOWN figures) instead of the world origin: nothing pins a dancer near the origin — `slideFigure`, `stepFigure`, `turnFigure` and the Move gizmo all translate `group.position` freely, and `orbit.maxDistance` is 10 m — so an origin-locked Front/Side/Top/¾ snapped to empty wood once a couple had walked, recoverable only by right-drag panning. It also calls `orbit.update()` so the aim is correct the instant it returns rather than on the next animation frame. "Frame dancers" (`app.frameDancers`, the button in `#view-buttons`) keeps the direction you are looking from and fits the shown dancers exactly, through `studio.fitPoints` — the clips' `frameCamera` fit, factored out and shared, so the two can never disagree. `studio.enterClip`/`exitClip` still save and restore the camera themselves and are untouched by any of this.
- **The sphere that lights is the sphere the click will use** (`clickTargetJoint` in main.js). It resolves an endpoint to its parent (`hand_L` → `wrist_L`, `toe_R` → `toes_R`, `headTop` → `head`) and, in Drag-limb mode, a chain trigger to its effector — and BOTH the hover styling and `handleClick` go through it. They used to disagree twice over: the hover skipped past a non-actionable sphere to an actionable one behind it while the click took `hits[0]` and fell off the end of its if/else chain (a lit cursor, a dead click), and hovering a hand lit the HAND while clicking selected the WRIST. A click every sphere under it refuses now says which mode wants it instead of no-opping. None of this touches selection semantics: resolving a joint is still not editing it, and no path here calls `markEdit`.
- **Only the DIRECT user-edit paths report a clamp.** `Figure.clampJoint` returns the clamp magnitude (largest per-axis |before − after| in radians, 0 if nothing moved), and exactly two callers act on it: the rotate gizmo's `objectChange` and `app.editJoint`, both via `reportJointClamp` in main.js. Do NOT wire the others — `solveTwoBone`/`swivelLimb`/`flattenFoot` in ik.js, `groundPlantedLeg`/`pivotHips` in main.js, the embrace's shoulder clamp — clamp on every iteration as a normal part of converging, so reporting there produces a permanent strobing message. The same rule covers the clamps that already return their applied amount: `moveHips` (returns the applied `dy`, and latches WHICH planted foot ran out of relevé in `app.hipsRiseLimit` — it latches because the successful back-off re-grounds that foot, so reading it afterwards always says "nobody") and `pivotHips` (returns the applied yaw; `trunkTwistBudget` re-derives the quoted ~43° from the same chest/spine `y` limits the clamp itself uses, so the number can never drift from the behaviour). Both clamps are correct — the feedback is the only thing that was missing.
- **The `view` block is OPT-IN, and that is load-bearing.** `getCoupleState`'s default shape is ALSO every undo snapshot (`pushHistory`), every COG-trail sample (`updateCogTrail`, ~289 rebuilds per edit) and every sequence keyframe (`seqAdd`/`seqUpdate`). Capturing the view by default would make an undo of a joint nudge also move the camera and restore old labels, and would serialise a label set 289 times per trail rebuild. Only the slide library passes `{ view: true }`. Conversely `applyCoupleState` restores a view only when the state carries one, so a pose-only state — every file saved before slides existed, plus A/B snapshots, keyframes and undo entries — leaves the layer, camera and labels exactly where the user has them. `dev-verify-slides.mjs` asserts both directions.
- **Present mode owns the keyboard in the CAPTURE phase.** The arrow and Page keys are already bound to nudging a joint or a figure, so a presenter stepping through slides would otherwise deform a dancer mid-lesson. The handler in main.js is registered with `useCapture = true` and calls `stopPropagation`, so no bubble-phase handler (nudges, Esc, Ctrl+Z) sees a key while presenting — and hands them all back on exit. `dev-verify-present.mjs` checks both halves: that stepping the deck leaves the pose byte-identical to the slide, and that the arrows nudge again afterwards.
- **A VIDEO IS A SLIDE THAT MOVES, so `app.recordPlayback` records from inside Present mode.** The joint pick spheres were the reported symptom, but they are one item on the list Present already takes off a slide — gizmos, a half-drawn annotation, a drawing's endpoint handles, a pending pin marker, the Label-mode cursor preview — and entering also forces the 16:9 1920×1080 frame an export is composed in. **The entry is synchronous in the click path for two independent reasons**: `enterPresent` asks for fullscreen, which a browser grants only from a user gesture, and it resizes the render target, which `studio.startRecorder` reads ONCE (`gl.width`) when it sizes its composite canvas — enter after the `whenEncoderReady()` await and the file opens at the old size. `app.recPresent` (`{ wasPresenting }`) is set only when null, so the MP4→WebM retry, which RE-ENTERS `recordPlayback`, stays inside the Present mode it is already holding rather than bouncing out and back in mid-capture; `finishRecPresent` runs on every terminal path (a finished take, a `startRecorder` that returned null, a retry that refused to start) and gives back exactly what the capture found. **Stopping is part of the same rule**: Present hides the ⏺ button, so Esc is the only stop within reach, and `app.stopRecording()` ends the take and **SAVES** it (`MediaRecorder.stop()` flushes through the normal `onstop` download). Only a job still ARMING is cancelled with no file, because an unwarmed H.264 session is zero bytes (see `warmUpMp4`). One Esc for the recording, a second for the presentation; every other presenter key is swallowed while a capture runs — a remote's click must not swap the slide mid-video — and `fullscreenchange` stops-and-saves before exiting, or a recorder is left running against a canvas that has changed shape. The "Recording… Esc stops and saves" notice is re-asserted per frame from the render loop (free: an identical `(text, kind)` touches no DOM) and, being page chrome, cannot reach the file. **Still leaking, knowingly**: `pins.group` (contact-pin end markers and strain lines) is dropped by `hideGizmos` for photos but NOT by Present, so it reaches a recorded sequence and a Present-mode slide. Gate: `dev-verify-record-present.mjs`.
- **A playback is stoppable, and stop is a PAUSE.** Both players (`app.playSeq` / `app.playInterp`) toggle: the button reads "■ Stop" while it runs, the dancers hold the pose they stopped in, `seqT`/`interpT` keep that position, and Play carries on from it (`resumeT` in main.js). `onDone` means "the movement finished" and is DROPPED by a stop, never fired. **Every path that clears a running flag goes through `clearPlaying`**, which is what notifies `ui.onPlaybackChanged` (→ `syncPlayButtons`, the ONE place either button's label/disabled state is decided); clearing `seqPlaying`/`interpPlaying` by hand leaves a button offering to stop something that is not running. Anything that would FIGHT the player frame by frame stops it — a scrub (before this, the grabbed thumb was yanked out from under the cursor every frame), a preset, a keyframe's Show, a slide, an undo (all at the `applyCoupleState` + `applyPreset` seams), Escape (first in `cancelPending`'s order), and the first edit of a dancer (hung on `markEdit`) — while merely LOOKING never does: an orbit or zoom is what a viewer does to watch the movement better. A per-frame CONSTRAINT must therefore never reach `markEdit`, or the player stops itself. Space is the toggle in Present mode and in the editor, where it is inert while focus sits in the sidebar/topbar or any field (a caption is full of spaces, and a focused button already treats Space as its own click). Gate: `dev-verify-playback-stop.mjs`.
- **`studio.js` MEASURES the sidebar's width, never assumes it.** `sidebarWidth()` reads the live element (a hidden one reports 0, which is exactly right) and falls back to the `--sidebar-w` token. Present mode hides the sidebar, and the old hardcoded `320` would have letterboxed the 16:9 frame against a panel that is not on screen. `--sidebar-w` is the single source for `#topbar`'s right inset and `#sidebar`'s width.
- **Selecting a joint is NOT editing it — it must never move anyone.** Which dancer yields to the embrace and collision (the partner of whoever is being edited) is decided by `embraceEditing()` in main.js, and it keys off `app.lastEditedFigure` / `editStamp` (set by `app.markEdit`), NOT off `app.selected`. This is deliberate: `embraceEditing` used to return `app.selected` outright, so merely CLICKING a joint on one dancer flipped who yields and jolted the partner on a click that changed no pose — measured, selecting the leader's elbow spun the follower's shoulder up to 220° and slid her body 376 mm. Clicking a joint to look at or start posing it is the single most common action here, so it is inert. Every path that ACTUALLY changes a pose calls `markEdit` (the gizmo `objectChange`, `editJoint`, `applyHandleChange`, `slideFigure`/`turnFigure`/`pivotHips`/`moveHips`/`setPelvisHeight`/`stepFigure`, and `setHandCurl`); a new mutation path must too, or the constraints won't know whose partner to move. Between edits the *figure* is remembered (so a drag ending doesn't hand the couple back to the default mover and jolt them again) but the *joint* is dropped (so an arm isn't left exempt from the embrace after the user let go); `applyPreset` clears it, since a preset places both dancers and no one is mid-edit. A verification that simulates a move by writing `group.position` directly (bypassing every `markEdit` path) must call `app.markEdit` itself to stand in for the real move — see `dev-verify-collision.mjs`'s edited-push case.
- **Selecting a label target is not editing either.** Label mode only reads the scene (`Labels.pick`) — it never calls `markEdit`, so naming a bone can't move anyone.
- **A second tap is counted, not taken from `dblclick`.** The pill's own drag already owns `pointerdown`/`pointerup` over a callout, so the second press arrives there and `labelTap` (400 ms, same label, no travel) is what opens the colour picker. The native `dblclick` event was tried first and is a dead end for verification: under CDP neither `page.mouse.click(..., { clickCount: 2 })` nor two separate clicks fire one on the canvas — measured, zero in both cases, with the `click` events themselves arriving normally. Counting it ourselves also means the gesture does not depend on how a browser decides to synthesise a double click, and it cannot double-fire against the drag.
- **On the clip stage a click means "this muscle", over the title it means "the title", and over a callout it means "that callout".** Both are reads, not edits (neither calls `markEdit`). A lit belly under the cursor takes the click on the clip stage only, and never in Label mode — off the stage that same click still picks a joint, and in Label mode it names the muscle; it must be LIT and it must be the FIRST muscle the ray meets, because you can only recolour what you can see. The title's grab is armed by HOVER (`titleHover` in main.js turns `orbit.enabled` off while the cursor is over the block), NOT at pointerdown: OrbitControls listens on the same canvas and would already have started a camera rotate by the time our own pointerdown handler ran. `orbit.enabled` is therefore ASSIGNED on every pointermove rather than toggled on the edge — a gizmo drag that ends under the title re-enables orbiting behind our back. A pointerup that ended a title drag returns before `handleClick`, so the gesture never reaches the scene.
- **Exports composite two canvases.** Labels and clip overlays live on the 2D overlay canvas, so a picture of the WebGL canvas alone is missing them. Never `toDataURL` / `captureStream` the GL canvas directly; go through `studio.photoDataURL` / `studio.startRecorder`. And never hand the user a video without `whenEncoderReady()` — an unwarmed MP4 is a silent 0-byte file.
- **Overlay sizes are fractions of the frame height, never pixels**, so a slide looks the same at any export resolution. A new overlay element must follow suit.
- **Feet are free.** Feet may point, rise to demi-pointe, or lift off the floor. Never add logic that forces feet down; only floor *penetration* is blocked (`clampToFloor` lifts the whole dancer just enough, and settles back only by undoing its own lifts). "Feet to floor" is an explicit opt-in button (`app.groundFeet`); it also flattens the toes joints. Demi-pointe: point the ankles (x>0) and extend the toes (`toes_L/R` x<0) so the toe pads stay flat on the floor. The one deliberate exception: keyframe interpolation grounds a foot that is planted at BOTH ends of the segment (it holds its spot or glides along the floor — see `groundInterpFeet` in main.js); a foot airborne at either end still lerps freely, so kicks and boleos fly.
- **Open vs. closed kinetic chain** is a core teaching feature: open chain rotates everything below the joint (ordinary FK); closed chain keeps the distal end (planted foot / joined hand) fixed and moves the body above. **Selecting a joint always starts in open chain** — closed chain moves the BODY rather than the limb, which surprises you if you didn't ask for it, so it stays a deliberate opt-in on the legs/pelvis via the joint panel's toggle (there is no auto-detect from foot contact; that was removed). The anchor is picked automatically from `ANCHOR_FOR`; the toes joint anchors itself, so extending it in closed chain is a relevé (heel rises over the pinned ball of the foot). The pelvis hip-height slider is special-cased (`app.setPelvisHeight`): rigid root compensation cancels a pure translation exactly, so in closed chain the legs re-solve via IK instead — planted feet keep their place and sole orientation while the body sinks or rises.
- **A figure's facing is `figureYaw(figure)`, never `group.rotation.y`.** They agree only while nothing has written the group's quaternion — and the Move-mode rotate gizmo writes it every pointermove. Three's XYZ Euler decomposition of a pure yaw past 90° comes back as `[180°, 180° − yaw, 180°]`: the same orientation, but `rotation.y` then reads a *different angle* and, worse, **runs backwards when incremented** (Euler `[180, θ, 180]` = `R_y(180 − θ)`, so `rotation.y += d` subtracts d from the real facing). Measured: a gizmo turn to 103.6° reported 76.4°, and the missing 27° went straight into the turn's pivot compensation as 111 mm of support-foot drift — with the pivot arithmetic itself provably correct, which is why it read as a mystery. `figureYaw` reads the yaw off the quaternion; `canonicalizeYaw` puts the rotation back in `[0, yaw, 0]` form (lossless — the orientation is a pure yaw either way) and is called at turn-drag start and on every `objectChange`, so every OTHER consumer of `rotation.y` (arrow-key turns, `rotateAbout`/`pivotFigure`, preset placement, pose save/load) stays correct without each having to know. Anything new that writes a figure's quaternion must canonicalize too.
- **Joint limits are enforced everywhere** — gizmo, sliders, IK, and presets must all stay within the ranges in `skeletonDef.js`.
- **Two frames, two jobs — pick deliberately.** `Figure.worldPos(joint)` is the RIG joint centre: Drillis–Contini anthropometry, and the right input for **biomechanics** (`computeCOG` lerps de Leva segment masses between exactly these points; the joint-angle and balance readouts are defined on them). `Figure.surfacePos(joint)` / `surfaceNode(joint)` is the node the **visible body** is welded to — the atlas node on a seated limb joint, the same rig node elsewhere — and is the right input for **contact**: collision volumes, pin spots, anything asking where the dancer physically is. The difference is not subtle, measured across six poses: the visible palm sits 165 mm from the rig wrist and wanders 107 mm inside it as the arm flexes, but sits a steady 39 mm from the ATLAS wrist and wanders **0.0 mm**; the elbow is 97 mm / 111 mm against 37 mm / 15 mm. A capsule or pin built on the rig node is displaced from its own limb by more than that limb's radius. `dev-verify-frames.mjs` reports both (`rigDrift`/`nodeOff` vs `atlasDrift`/`atlasOff`), so this is checkable rather than assumed.
- **A spot stored in one frame must be replayed in that same frame.** `pins.js`'s `spotNode()` is the single rule deciding which frame a pin end lives in, and `nearestJointNode`, main.js's authoring, `endWorld` and `maintainLimbs` all route through it. Storing a click in one frame and playing it back in another puts the pin somewhere the user never clicked — measured at ~10 cm on a flexed arm. Also: `solveTwoBone` writes RIG rotations, so any loop that re-reads an atlas-framed spot between solver passes must `syncAtlasNodes()` first, or the residual correction reads the previous iteration's pose and diverges (the same trap documented for the embrace's `handCenter`).
- **THE ARM CHAIN IS COLLAPSED ONTO THE ANATOMICAL JOINT CENTRES, AND THE EMBRACE IS KNOWINGLY BROKEN PENDING REWORK.** The arm rig node and the anatomical node are now the same point, so the arm has ONE frame: `palm_center`'s drift inside its own rig node went 107 mm → **0**, `elbow_skin` 111 → 17 mm, and `handMesh`'s old split (orientation measured in the rig wrist, position stored in the atlas wrist) is gone. Everything else holds — calibration matches, preset grounding unchanged (`low` ≈ 0), collision, pins (arm 0.6 cm), muscles including the bi-articular biceps/triceps skinning, and the foot fits. **The embrace does not**, and that was an accepted, deliberate trade (it needed rework anyway). Its exact failing state, as the baseline to fix against: `close embrace` clasp gap **3.8 cm** (want 1.4) and closed-side palms off by **L 17.6 cm** (the follower's side of this is FIXED — she now rests on his back at 0.2–0.4 cm settled, 5.3 cm in this transient, via the sliding `CLOSED_TARGETS` rest point and `#reachClamp`; the leader's 17.6 is the part still outstanding, and the suite is at least DETERMINISTIC now — it used to return 1.7, 16.9 and 50.9 cm on three identical runs of this same case); after a 60° pivot, clasp gap **11.6 cm** with the leader's clasp fingers **61°** off the tilt direction. **Fix `#solveArm`, not `embraceArms`** — re-authoring those angles for the new rest was measured to move the closed-side error only 17.8 → 17.6 cm, because the closed-side arms are IK-solved and the authored angles merely seed the swivel; the real gap is the solve against the shoulder's new position (15 mm medial, 15 mm lower). The other consequence of the new rest is already handled: it is **abducted ~9.1° with a 19.4° elbow bend** where the old rest hung straight down and collinear, which broke `solveTwoBone`'s assumption that `rotation.x = 0` is full extension — `REST_BEND` in `ik.js` derives each hinge's rest bend from `JOINTS` and subtracts it (a no-op on the still-collinear legs).
- **Arms are migrated to surface nodes; legs deliberately are not.** Both `collision.js` (arm capsules) and `pins.js` (arm spots) resolve through `surfaceNode`; legs stay on rig nodes even though they are seated too. Legs are coupled to things the arms are not — `clampToFloor` and the balance hull read the sole corners in the RIG ankle's frame, and the `COLLIDERS` radii are calibrated so the shipped contact presets rest at ~0 clearance. Driving a leg in the atlas frame fights the floor clamp (measured: it lifted a pinned foot from 9 mm off the floor to 66 mm). Migrating legs means moving the corner tables with them and re-verifying grounding — a separate change, not an oversight.
- **Measure the hand the VIEWER sees, never the rig node.** The clothed hand is welded to the *atlas* wrist (`Figure.palmPosWorld` / `palmDirWorld` / `fingerDirWorld`); the rig `wrist_*`/`hand_*` nodes agree with it in rotation but drift up to ~18 cm from it in position as the arm flexes. Constraints solve the rig chain — that is correct, it is the IK effector — but every *goal* and every *measurement* must be mesh truth, with the residual between them corrected each pass. The cautionary tale: an embrace solved entirely on rig nodes reported a perfect 2.2 cm palm-to-palm clasp, backed by a green verification suite, while the rendered hands hung 15 cm apart in mid-air — a suite built on rig nodes could not see it in principle, and no amount of tuning inside the solve (pronation range, swivel search, lean ranking) could either, because the objective pointed at a hand that was not being drawn. Verification of anything touching a hand must read the avatar's own bones (`fingerBones`), not the rig. The same divergence is worth suspecting anywhere else limb *positions* are read off rig nodes on a flexed limb — arm collision capsules and arm contact pins are unaudited on this point.
- **The dancers may touch but never occupy each other's space** (`collision.js`). Walking, kicking or dragging one dancer into the other displaces the partner — that is the point (a leader entering the follower's space moves her, like a real sacada) — and the edited dancer always keeps their ground. Never "fix" a pose by letting bodies overlap: if a contact pose won't reach, either the pose is dishonest (e.g. heads nodding through each other — fix the pose, see the Walk preset's neck angles) or a `COLLIDERS` radius is too fat. Close embrace pulls to `CONTACT_FRACTION` *or* until bodies rest surface-on-surface, whichever comes first — after a pivot the couple may legitimately rest foot-against-foot farther than chest contact.
- **A contact pin holds spots together, never through each other.** Pins adapt the partner of whoever is being edited (arm/leg pins re-solve that limb within its joint limits, torso pins slide the dancer horizontally); collision still wins, so a pin whose spots sit inside the partner's volume legitimately rests at surface contact with a small residual spot gap — don't "fix" that by disabling collision, and an out-of-reach pin shows its amber strain line instead of teleporting anyone. A pin on an embrace arm overrides the embrace hold for that arm by design (`maintainLimbs` runs after `maintainHands`).
- **A posed arm can't pass through the partner's body, but the embrace arms rest freely on it.** The arm being actively posed is a collider against the partner's core, so pushing it into them displaces them (never "fix" this by letting the arm spear through); but arms are colliders *only while being posed* — the embrace and presets rest arms ON and AROUND the partner by design, so making them collide otherwise shoves the couple apart (which is why this is edit-scoped, not always-on — do not "simplify" it to always-on). The hand is never a collider (the embrace rests it on the partner).
- Pose-authoring gotcha: pelvis x-tilt or deep toe-points can push a foot below the floor, which makes the clamp lift the dancer and ruins the balance stats (the support foot floats and the weight reading lands on the wrong foot). Author trailing/pointed feet with hip/knee/ankle angle triples whose toe grazes y ≈ 0 — don't derive them by hand; probe candidate angles with `scripts/dev-tune.mjs` until `low` ≈ 0 and the standing sole is flat at 0. Known-good triples at standing pelvis heights: trailing grazing toe `hip 15 / knee 36 / ankle 33` (pelvisY 0.527), collected free foot beside the support foot `hip 2 / knee 50 / ankle 42` (pelvisY 0.525).
